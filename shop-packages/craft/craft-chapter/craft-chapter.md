## Light

Job: cut the reading experience so each stop invites the next page — without cheating.

Contract: every chapter ending makes an implicit promise the next chapter will address.

Must-not-break:
- Scene = story logic unit; chapter = pacing/pause container (can split mid-scene)
- Prefer hooks most of the time; cliffhangers sparingly
- End at the inhale (wanting), not after the full exhale (permission to stop)
- Forgotten promises / evaporating tension = cheat
- Vary ending types; uniform endings numb
- Length ≠ value — cut dead weight; don’t pad to “fill” a chapter

## Normal

**Scene vs chapter**
- Scene: time/place, want, interference, outcome — something different at end
- Chapter: where reader pauses or feels pull — not the unit of story logic
- One chapter may hold one scene or several unified around one dramatic movement
- Revision lens: what each *scene* does for the central problem — stop protecting words for length

**Hooks vs cliffhangers**
- **Hook** — curiosity about what’s ahead without interrupting the current moment
- **Cliffhanger** — cut mid-action / mid-reveal purely to withhold
- Rough balance often cited: ~80% hooks / ~15% strategic cliffs / ~5% breathable closure
- Cheap tricks: false alarm next page; cut mid-sentence for no reason; withhold info the POV already has; random danger with no setup

**Ending modes**
- Cliffhanger — crisis before resolution
- Revelation — new info that reframes; must matter to central story
- Resonant close — emotional crystallization; still leave a larger thread open

**Hook toolkit:** question decision revelation threat promise emotion reversal escalation

**Length / rhythm**
- Short chapters accelerate; long chapters immerse — must earn length
- Vary length; scene breaks = mini-endings at lower intensity

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| False cliffhanger | Cost or consequence next page |
| Completing the local situation | Cut earlier; leave a thread |
| Max intensity every ending | Mix resonant / revelation |
| Forgotten threads | Ending audit list |
| Same ending every chapter | Force three alternative ending types |

**Draft exercises**
- Ending audit: one line per chapter end — variety? would you turn?
- Three alternatives: cliff / revelation / resonant
- Seed earlier the detail that makes the last line click

## Hard

**Serial history → novel chapter cuts**
- Cliffhanger as retention device: Victorian serials (Dickens instalments), film serials, modern multi-POV / thriller chapter cuts
- Two retention engines: (1) life-threatening / precarious situation; (2) sudden shocking revelation
- Serial “take-outs” that cheat by rewriting the peril in the recap are the ancestor of false-alarm chapter tricks — readers notice

**Zeigarnik / unfinished business (with caveat)**
- Classic claim: unfinished / interrupted tasks stay more accessible in memory than completed ones — craft talk maps this onto chapter cuts
- Replication is contested; meta-analysis debate exists — use as *reader-experience hypothesis*, not lab law
- Practical: leave a specific unfinished cognitive loop (question, decision, threat) — not generic “danger!”

**When to break the hook habit**
- Resonant closes and rest-stop chapters earn trust between peaks — constant cliffs numb (same logic as Hitchcock “bumps” scheduling)
- Multi-POV switch after a cliff: valid but mean; pay the emotional invoice soon or readers feel toyed with
- Book/series sequel hooks that resolve the immediate act while leaving trilogy arcs open ≠ mid-action cheat if the chapter’s local job completed
