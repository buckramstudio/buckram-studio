# Tension and suspense craft

Reader-state craft beyond thriller genre packaging. md` (devices/clock) and (cost of failure).

## Light

Job: keep pages turning via felt need to know what happens next — even in quiet scenes.

Contract: conflict enables suspense; suspense enables tension; tension is the felt pull.

Must-not-break:
- Care first — no investment → zero tension
- Micro-tension in “nothing happening” scenes
- Partial answers that raise new questions
- Relentless max tension numbs — need rhythm
- Every open question is a contract (payoff or trust dies)
- Not genre-locked (romance, literary, epic all use it)

## Normal

**Tension vs suspense vs conflict**
- **Conflict** = opposition (want vs obstacle) — the *what*
- **Suspense** = state of not knowing — the *question*
- **Tension** = felt need to know what happens next — the *how it feels*

**Micro-tension (Maass)**
- Moment-by-moment apprehension about the *next few seconds*, not only the big plot
- Sources: conflicting emotions in one beat; unspoken feelings; intra-character conflicting desires; slightly wrong environment; sticky surprising phrasing
- Exercise: character at rest → less-obvious place details → primary feeling + simultaneous conflicting feeling → weave sensory substance around emotional subject
- Prose pathway (Lakin): mood-coded imagery that raises questions without stating the crime

**Unanswered questions + delay / reveal**
- Raise a specific question → make them wait
- Sweet spot: **partial answers that raise new questions**
- Answer too fast → deflates; starve too long → frustration
- Info-gap toolkit: 

**Silence, clock, care, rhythm**
- Speed → excitement; slowness / silence → dread
- Ticking clock (literal or figurative) under quiet scenes
- Breathers still need micro-tension (never empty)
- Promise chain: unanswered question + escalation

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Quiet scene with no micro-friction | Conflicting emotion + sticky detail |
| Instant answers | Partial reveal + new question |
| Max tension forever | Release → deeper tension |
| Perfect clock, zero care | Invest relationship first |

## Hard

**Dread vs surprise (dramatic irony)**
- **Dramatic irony / dread:** reader knows more than character → anticipation; builds through waiting
- **Surprise:** reader and character learn together → release via reveal; must be earned by prior clues
- Choose by desired feel: worry → irony; gasp → surprise
- Genre lean (tendency): thriller/horror/tragedy often irony; mystery/comedy/twist psych often surprise
- Layer: irony that friend is betrayer + later surprise that betrayal was protective
- Hitchcock bomb-under-table: knowing danger > sudden explosion for sustained suspense

**Tension audit (draft pass)**
1. Five consecutive scenes: after each, what question is the reader asking? None → weak forward pull
2. Breathers: quieter vs dead zones
3. Escalation curve: midpoint > opening; climax > midpoint?
4. Any dramatic irony beats?
5. Last line of each chapter: open question / shift / hook — or full resolution (exit ramp)?
