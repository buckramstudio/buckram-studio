# Show, don’t tell

Traditional craft: when explanation steals experience. AI “humanize” lists are related but separate — see / . Filter words / sensory immersion: . Film-rule misuse vs prose interiority: .

## Light

Job: help readers experience the story — reserve telling for shortcuts between dramatized peaks.

Contract: show important turns; summarize the connective tissue.

Must-not-break:
- Don’t explain motive/emotion/subtext the scene already implies
- Embodied action + sparse internalization > named feelings stacks
- Constant showing exhausts; peaks need valleys (tell as bridge)
- Dramatize important scenes; summarize between
- Rigid “show only” can bias against some cultural/immigrant tell modes
- Specific sensory/body detail > generic “she was sad”

## Normal

**Three common tell patterns (Janice Hardy)**
1. **Explaining motives** — “Doing X because Y” / why before the reader wonders. Red flags: `to [verb]`, `because`, `knew`
2. **Explaining emotions behind actions** — naming the feeling instead of embodied signs. Red flags: `felt`, `in [emotion]`, `with [emotion]`
3. **Explaining subtext** — internalization that restates what dialogue/action already imply

**Internal conflict without essay**
- Internal cues: circling thoughts (don’t drown pace); avoidance/denial; vacillating options
- External cues: over-control vs deferring; distraction; uncharacteristic mistakes; volatility matched to personality

**Classical framing**
- Reader experiences via actions, words, subtext, thoughts, senses — not author analysis
- Chekhov tradition (paraphrase): don’t say the moon shines — show glint on broken glass
- Hemingway iceberg / omission: leave much unsaid if the writing is true enough
- Palahniuk tip (use judiciously): ban “thought verbs” (*thinks, knows, realizes, wants…*) in favor of sensory/action detail

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Motive lecture | Imply via behavior; cut `because` |
| Emotion labels | Body + sparse thought |
| Subtext restated | Strip internalization |
| Show-everything slog | Tell bridges between peaks |
| Generic sadness | Specific thesaurus detail  |

## Hard

**Named exceptions (craft consensus)**
- Bell: constant showing exhausts readers and flattens peaks — tell as shortcut between meaty scenes
- Card: dramatize important scenes; summarize between — balance rhythm/pace/tone
- Dialogue can show (Lavandier) — talk as action, not only speech labels
- Nguyen critique: rigid “show only” workshops can bias against some cultural/immigrant narrative modes that use tell legitimately

**Medium bridge**
- Film “show don’t tell” is camera-bound; prose interiority is allowed and often required — don’t import film bans wholesale 
- Exposition timing still matters: curiosity valve before lore 

**When telling is the right tool**
- Time jumps and logistics that would bore if dramatized
- Emotional rest beats after high show density
- Voice-driven tell (ironic narrator, oral tradition stance) when the *telling* is the experience
