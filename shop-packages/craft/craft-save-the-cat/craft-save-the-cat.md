# Save the Cat / beat sheet for novels

Blake Snyder’s screenwriting beat sheet (*Save the Cat!*, 2005) adapted for novelists by Jessica Brody (*Save the Cat! Writes a Novel*, 2018). Prescriptive % waypoints for commercial storytelling — useful for planning *and* diagnosing sagging middles. Distill only; do not paste proprietary worksheets wholesale.

## Light

Job: place emotional/structural shifts readers expect so the middle does not sag into random events.

Contract: sequence of *jobs* matters more than exact page %; structure, not fill-in-the-blank quality.

Must-not-break:
- Catalyst → Break into Two → Midpoint → All Is Lost → Break into Three (five foundation beats)
- Fun and Games delivers promise of the premise — not fluff
- Midpoint is false victory *or* false defeat; stakes spike
- Opening Image ↔ Final Image bookend the arc
- “Save the Cat” early humanizing action = likeability craft, not a required numbered beat
- Flex % for genre; keep order of jobs

## Normal

**Fifteen beats (paraphrased jobs + ~%)**

Percentages ≈ share of manuscript. For ~80k words, 1% ≈ 800 words. Sequence > exact %.

### Act 1 (~0–20%)

| # | Beat | ~% | Job (paraphrase) |
|---|------|----|------------------|
| 1 | Opening Image | 0–1 | “Before” snapshot — tone, world, starting state |
| 2 | Theme Stated | ~5 | Someone (often not hero) voices thematic question |
| 3 | Set-Up | 1–10 | Flaw, status-quo stakes, cast; what’s missing |
| 4 | Catalyst | ~10 | Life knocked off axis |
| 5 | Debate | 10–20 | Should I engage? Resist / prepare / argue |

### Act 2 (~20–80%)

| # | Beat | ~% | Job (paraphrase) |
|---|------|----|------------------|
| 6 | Break into Two | ~20 | Choice into upside-down world |
| 7 | B Story | ~22 | Relationship that carries theme / teaches Truth |
| 8 | Fun and Games | 20–50 | Promise of the premise |
| 9 | Midpoint | ~50 | False win *or* false defeat; stakes spike |
| 10 | Bad Guys Close In | 50–75 | External + internal pressure (mirrors midpoint polarity) |
| 11 | All Is Lost | ~75 | Rock bottom; “whiff of death” (literal or metaphorical) |
| 12 | Dark Night of the Soul | 75–80 | Emotional aftermath before new plan |

### Act 3 (~80–100%)

| # | Beat | ~% | Job (paraphrase) |
|---|------|----|------------------|
| 13 | Break into Three | ~80 | Aha — plan that fixes Act 2 *and* the self |
| 14 | Finale | 80–99 | Execute; prove theme; threads converge |
| 15 | Final Image | 99–100 | “After” snapshot mirroring Opening Image |

**Five foundation beats (start here):** Catalyst → Break into Two → Midpoint → All Is Lost → Break into Three.

**Novelist notes**
- Fun and Games ≠ fluff — longest stretch; escalate while delivering genre pleasure
- Midpoint polarity: false win → Bad Guys trends down; false loss → external “up” while flaws still close in
- B Story often bridges Dark Night → Break into Three
- Flex %: literary may stretch Set-Up; thriller may compress Debate

| Situation | Prefer |
|-----------|--------|
| Lost in middle | STC Fun and Games → Midpoint → Bad Guys |
| % map for commercial fiction | STC or Weiland |
| Designing from one sentence | Snowflake then overlay STC |
| Scene-level value change | Story Grid  |
| After draft | Reverse outline + STC labels |

## Hard

**Thesis / antithesis / synthesis + Board**
- Snyder groups acts as thesis → antithesis → synthesis; BS2 specifies the fifteen shifts inside that skeleton
- Set-up should surface “things that need fixing” (Snyder’s “Six Things” — number arbitrary)
- Planning tool: The Board — four rows (Act 1 / Act 2a / Act 2b / Act 3) with scene cards marked for emotional change + conflict

**Immutable-laws cousins (paraphrase only)**
- **Save the Cat** (namesake): early action that makes audience root for the hero
- **Pope in the Pool**: bury necessary exposition inside a diverting visual/action beat so info does not stall

**Genre categories ≠ shelf genres**
- Snyder’s story types (Monster in the House, Golden Fleece, Dude with a Problem, Buddy Love, Whydunit, etc.) classify *engine*, not bookstore aisle — useful when “romance” or “thriller” is too coarse for structure diagnosis

**Reception / when to break**
- Widespread adoption → risk of beat-sheet sameness (Suderman / *Slate*: industry sticking to exact order and page counts)
- Pre-STC hits often contain the *jobs* out of proportion — use as diagnostic, not sacred page math
- Critiques of gendered “guy who…” hero framing exist in film-studies reception — choose hero for maximum conflict + longest emotional journey, not default gender
- Pair with Weiland % map when you need Inciting↔Climax question logic without STC names
