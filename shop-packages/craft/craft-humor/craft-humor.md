## Light

Job: make laughs intentional — incongruity with a story job — not decorative parsley.

Contract: funniest word lands at sentence end; joke serves character, tension, or truth; tonal range established early.

Must-not-break:
- Expectation → surprising subversion that feels inevitable (“of course”)
- End-stress: punchline at sentence/beat end
- Character-specific humor > interchangeable witticisms
- Don’t explain the joke
- After devastation, don’t always release immediately 

## Normal

**Core mechanic: incongruity + resolution**
- Gap between expectation and reality; sharper turn → stronger laugh (often one word or tone shift)
- Craft skill, not personality trait — architecture of surprise, word by word
- Purpose first: break tension, deepen character, make a hard truth absorbable
- Random = distracts; intentional = sharpens; decorative = parsley

**Comic timing on the page**
**End-stress (non-negotiable):** funniest word/phrase at sentence end.
- Wrong: “He had the look of a man who had recently been punched by his own expectations about life.”
- Right: “He had the look of a man whose expectations about life had recently punched him.”

Other levers:
- After long setup → short sentence / fragment detonates
- Extra words weaken impact; cut around setup/payoff
- Short after long = slap cadence
- Comic cut: juxtapose contradictory beats so irony is felt, not explained
- Interruption comedy: punchline is preventing the punchline

Edit check: cover last word — if joke still lands without it, humor was front-loaded; restructure.

**Specificity and diction**
- Vague kills comedy; particular nouns land harder
- Concrete > abstract; Latinate diction in lowbrow situation = incongruity
- Misapplied register: consumer review for marriage; bureaucratic minutes for gnome wars
- Defamiliarization: outsider describes ordinary rituals neutrally → absurdity from accuracy

**Structural joke engines**
**Rule of three:** two items set register; third violates it.
- Flat: “warm beds, hot meals, and reasonable rates”
- Comic: “…and a philosophical debate about whether rats counted as pets”

Other: escalation of absurd-but-plausible decisions status reversal / earnestness vs scale expert who can’t self-apply straight man + chaos agent format comedy (wrong framework)

**Character-based humor**
- Joke only this character would notice/care about too much
- Funny because of who they are — sincere Carrot in cynical world; Wooster’s goodwill + denseness
- Joke-machine characters kill surprise
- Dramatic irony: narrator belief vs truth 
- Real stakes: want + something to lose → failures become funny
- Humor texture can shift while origin stays constant; defense mechanism the story may dismantle

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Explains the joke | Fix setup or cut |
| Front-loaded punch | End-stress rewrite |
| Interchangeable wit | Character-specific notice |
| Laugh after devastation cheats | Hold dark; later valve  |
| Italics as comedy | Rewrite; don’t underline |

**Revision pass**
1. Name the incongruity spine
2. End-stress check
3. Cut non-working words
4. Read aloud for drag
5. Delete anything after punchline that softens or explains

## Hard

**When humor undercuts stakes**
- After a devastating beat, immediate comic relief can cheat — sometimes stay in the dark; later laugh lands harder
- Not every scene needs funny; restraint = release after serious passage
- Tonal contract: establish range early; joke that violates chapter-15 contract jars
- Comic novels can hold grief/violence if readers know the world’s rules first — humor often teaches those rules
- Humor that serves goal pursuit = texture; humor that replaces cost = undercuts 
- Grief example that works: casserole multiplication at funerals — relief without competing with emotion

**Tone-contract diagnostic**
| Symptom | Cause | Move |
|---------|-------|------|
| Chapter-3 funny, ch-15 joke jars | Contract never set | Plant range early |
| Empty pratfall | No Want / stakes | Attach cost to failure |
| Joke-machine fatigue | Function not person | Cut witticism quota; add sincere beats |
| Satire read as mean without target | Unclear moral vector | Clarify what the joke *costs*  |

**When to break end-stress**
- Running gag may land mid-sentence once the pattern is trained — still prefer end-stress for new jokes
- Deadpan catalogs can accumulate mid-list absurdity — third-item rule still applies
