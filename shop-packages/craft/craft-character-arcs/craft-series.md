## Light

Job: give each volume a complete meal while the series question advances toward a tracked payoff.

Contract: book conflict is a microcosm of series conflict; promises planted get paid or explicitly retired; growth sticks across books.

Must-not-break:
- Book dramatic question answered inside the volume
- Series question advanced (finished only in finale)
- End each book: satisfaction + earned next-book promise (not pure cheat cliffhanger)
- Continuity bible: cast state, world rules, timeline, who-knows-what
- Escalation more personal/consequential — not only geographically bigger

## Normal

**Series types**

| Type | Pattern | Planning note |
|------|---------|---------------|
| Sequential | One continuous story; must read in order | Know ending + major joints before book 1 |
| Standalone-connected | Complete stories; shared world/cast | Flexibility; still need connective tissue |
| Episodic | Same protag, new challenge per book | Can run long; gradual character change |
| Episodic + series arc | Complete book plots + slow overarching threat | Most flexible commercial hybrid |

**Nested arcs**
- **Series arc** — overarching question / war / transformation answered only at the end
- **Book arc** — complete dramatic question answered inside the volume
- Book conflict should be a **microcosm** of series conflict
- Book that only advances series = published chapter; book that only local-resolves = filler for series readers

**Plan shape, draft books**
- Series plan: 5–10 pages — series question, protag journey, antagonists, start/end world-state, major turning points per book
- Book plan: scene-level as your method needs
- Avoid inverse: locked scenes for book 5 + improvised book structure
- Macro fixed / micro fluid (Sanderson-style discovery inside outline)

**Promise of the series**
Sanderson frame at series scale: **promise → progress → payoff**. Track each promise: when planted what promised intended payoff book status open/paid/retired.
Failure mode: **plot-promise drift** — unanswered threads pile up; readers leave ~book 4.

**Ending each book: satisfaction + hook**
1. Resolve book-level conflict (meal, not appetizer)
2. Escalate / reframe series stakes in Resolution

Strong pattern: reader thought problem was X → X resolved → ending reveals real problem is Y (worse). Avoid pure cliffhanger that resolves *nothing*. Book-1: satisfaction-first, then tease.
Per-book skeleton: Setup → Pressure → Midpoint turn → Dark moment → Climax + **next-book promise**.

**Continuity infrastructure**
Series compounds: world rules persist character state accumulates timeline load-bearing.
Bible minimum: characters world rules timeline/ages open promises who knows what deliberate retcons.
Prefer bible **derived from manuscript** — update when a detail is introduced/changed.

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Middle-book sag | Self-contained high-stakes arc + series reframe |
| Continuity collapse | Bible from MS; check ages/rules between books |
| Plot-promise drift | Ledger: pay or retire |
| Growth resets | New justified pressure if courage vanishes |
| Recap dumps | Weave reminders into new scenes |

**Series sweep checklist**
- [ ] Book DQ answered; series DQ advanced (until finale)
- [ ] Character growth consistent
- [ ] Ages / seasons / travel times math
- [ ] Magic/tech/culture rules stable unless paid for
- [ ] Promise ledger clean
- [ ] Emotional continuity matches history 

## Hard

**Trilogy / character arcs across books**
- Trilogy ≈ three-act shape at series scale — don’t force 1 act = 1 book exactly
- Series Act Two often starts late in book 1 and ends early in book 3
- Every book still needs its own three acts + answered dramatic question
- Optional: **mini-Lies** per book that facet the overarching Lie; book 3 focuses the full Lie
- Growth must stick: courage earned in book 2 can’t vanish in book 4 without new justified pressure

**Stakes & escalation pitfalls**
- Escalation should feel more personal/consequential, not only geographically bigger (personal → city → world → cosmos can lose emotional core)
- Middle books need their own climax + at least one revelation that reframes the series
- Final book: pay series question, arcs, antagonist, threads — complete, not abandoned (bittersweet OK)

**Type × promise diagnostic**
| Symptom | Likely cause | Move |
|---------|--------------|------|
| Book 2 feels optional | Sequential sold as episodic | Clarify type; give book 2 own DQ |
| Finale can’t land | Series question never planted in book 1 | Retroplant or rewrite opening promise |
| Standalone-connected drift | No connective tissue | Shared consequence / returning debt |
| Episodic flatness | No series arc pressure | Add slow overarching threat |

**When to break “no pure cliffhanger”**
- Serialized commercial expectation can license a harder cut *after* a real book payoff
- Still prefer earned new question over unresolved book climax
