# Backstory craft

When/how the past lands on the page — Ghost/wound without dump.

## Light

Job: give just enough past so present motivation and climax make sense — iceberg, not biography.

Contract: readers feel the Ghost’s pressure before they get the full wound story.

Must-not-break:
- Share only when readers need it for the *main* story (or plot cannot move)
- Suffocation = answering questions readers haven’t asked yet
- Prefer effect → cause; behavior *is* backstory until origin earns reveal
- Functional past ≠ full résumé
- Hint Ghost; delay full dump until emotional relevance

## Normal

**Core model**
- Backstory = what precedes the main narrative; creates depth even when mostly unstated
- Primary job: **context for motivation** — why this goal this hard
- **Ghost / Wound** (Truby / Weiland): past that haunts; installs or sustains the Lie; needs laying to rest
- Ghost need not be trauma — upbringing that never forced questioning still explains orientation
- Author may know everything; readers get only what affects the bottom line

**Dump patterns to relocate**
1. **Ch.1 résumé** — apartment tour + 800 words of brokenness before root-for action
2. **Flashback that kills charge** — confrontation → three pages in 1994 → tension gone
3. **History-vessel dialogue** — “As you know, since Dad never believed in you…”

**Delivery modes (scar before wound)**
1. Backstory drip — sentence/paragraph shards; default
2. Dialogue breadcrumbs — speak *around* history; no mutual-known recap
3. Sensory triggers — smell/sound flash via reaction, not formal scene
4. Scar without wound — flinch; cash always in wallet; won’t ask for help
5. Artifacts — cracked trophy, faded photo, junk-drawer museum
6. Revelation under pressure — cornered/high conflict drops the filter
7. Setting as ghost — return to renamed street / crumbling school
8. Unreliable account — conflicting tellings → past as puzzle
9. Formal excuse (brief) — interview, interrogation, first date; keep short
10. Lateral flashback — brief; answers a *present* problem; else distraction
11. Strategic prologue — tease “original sin” as Hook, not world-history dump
12. Fragment scatter — offhand detail → physical tell → later specific memory

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Sympathy dump before action | Cut; show pattern under pressure first |
| Interesting past, no present urgency | Attach reveal to a now-choice |
| Flashback as tourism | Require it change how current action is read |
| Keep because “cool lore” | If Climax still clear without it → bible only |

**Self-edit pass**
1. Highlight every pre-story explanation
2. One sentence: honest job (not “show trauma”)
3. Triage now / later / never (or convert to behavior)
4. If keep: tighten; attach to present pressure
5. Less is more

## Hard

**Flashbacks / dual timelines (Ghost-specific)**
- Canonical interrupt craft: 
- Interest ≠ past explosions; interest = **emotional urgency** equal to present timeline
- Alternating full backstory timeline only when past is equally dramatic, pertinent, and reader-hungry
- Earn dramatized Ghost reveal after drip has created want-to-know
- Delay until emotional relevance: origin of abandonment fear when she’s about to sabotage — after the pattern is watched

**Timing triage (not sympathy tests)**
Ask: does the reader need *this* info *right now* to care about what happens next?

Not sufficient alone:
- Will it make them sympathetic?
- Does it explain behavior?
- Is it interesting?

**When to break “delay the dump”**
- Strategic prologue that *is* the Hook (first spark / original sin) — still not a world-history dump
- Formal excuse scenes (interrogation, first date) tolerate brief summary if kept short and pressure-bound
- Unreliable conflicting accounts can withhold “truth” longer because the puzzle *is* the present plot
