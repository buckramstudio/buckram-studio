# Narrative voice

Narrator’s sound, distance, and worldview — not cast dialogue voice.

## Light

Job: control how close the reader sits to events and whose worldview the narrating layer carries.

Contract: distance and register feel intentional — intimacy ↔ release, not accidental MFA drift.

Must-not-break:
- Psychic distance is a spectrum, not on/off
- Flat-close or flat-far both dull; rhythm intimacy
- Leap 1→5 jars unless the jar is intentional
- Narrative voice ≠ spoken dialogue voice
- Accidental register breaks feel like author intrusion

## Normal

**Psychic / narrative distance (Gardner spectrum)**

| Level | Feel | Shape |
|-------|------|--------|
| 1 | Bird’s-eye / remote | Time, place, unnamed figure |
| 2 | Named, preference | “Henry had never much cared for snowstorms.” |
| 3 | Emotion named, narrator-led | “Henry hated snowstorms.” |
| 4 | Character diction bleeds in (FID) | “God how he hated these damn snowstorms.” |
| 5 | Inside / stream-of-consciousnessy | Fragments; sensory crush |

Craft uses:
- Far out: orient, summarize, bridge, clean POV change
- Mid (≈3–4): most contemporary fiction — involvement without claustrophobia
- Closest-in: peak emotion, altered states, or sustained style choice
- POV switch: walk out of A’s close levels into neutral space, then into B

**Free indirect discourse (FID)**

| Mode | Shape |
|------|--------|
| Direct | He thought, “And just what pleasure…?” |
| Indirect | He asked himself what pleasure… |
| **Free indirect** | And just what pleasure had he found…? |

Marks: drop reporting clauses/quotes; keep character deixis, curses, fragments; past-tense third; judgments backshifted into narrative tense.

**Voice as worldview**
- Selection: what gets named, skipped, joked about, moralized
- Metaphor habit: cynic’s weather ≠ mystic’s weather (same street)
- Irony gap: what focalizer believes vs what narration lets reader infer
- Test: strip character names — does worldview still sound like *this* book / focalizer?

**Register and consistency**
- Pick a band (street / plain / literary / period / comic) + slang/abstraction tolerance
- Consistency ≠ monotony: distance may slide; accidental register breaks shouldn’t
- FID diction spikes should feel *earned by closeness*
- Longform: lock a short narrator voice card (samples, banned “pretty” words, default sentence length, humor)

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Always stuck at 5 | Add orient/release beats |
| Sudden lyricism in hardboiled layer | Restore register band |
| FID unmarked mush | Mark start/stop; keep deixis consistent |
| Dialogue and narrator identical | Split cards  |

## Hard

**Why FID matters for narrative voice**
- Austen strength (Page via wiki): vividness of speech **without** total silencing of authorial irony — narrator can still shade
- “Free” = capacity to roam among centers of consciousness (not locked to one skull forever)
- Stevenson distinction sometimes used: free indirect *discourse* (aloud speech) vs free indirect *style* (silent thought)
- Practitioners often cited: Austen, Flaubert (*discours indirect libre*), Woolf, Joyce, Kafka (*erlebte Rede*)
- Don’t conflate FID with first person; stream of consciousness (more disordered) → 

**Irony / multi-POV edge cases**
- Omniscient narrator with a personality is still a *voice* (see )
- Multi-close-third usually changes voice with each POV
- Wanted confusion: reader may blur narrator judgment with character opinion — only if intentional

**Quick mastery checks**
- Does each major scene choose distance on purpose (orient → close → release)?
- Can you mark where FID starts/stops without guessing?
- Would a reader confuse narrator judgment with character opinion — and is that wanted?
