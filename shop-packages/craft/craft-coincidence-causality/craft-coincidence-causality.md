# Coincidence and causality

When chance is allowed — and when it must become cause.

## Light

Job: let accident start trouble; make solutions arise from prior action.

Contract: climactic rescue-by-luck is cousin to deus ex — usually damages internal logic.

Must-not-break:
- Problems may arrive by accident; solutions must be earned 
- Lucky break needs prior plant (skill, ally in position, world rule)
- Coincidence that *rescues* the climax = author panic until prepared
- Destiny/fate framing still needs agency and choice cost
- Comedy may pile coincidences *as the joke* if tone contract says so

## Normal

**Rule of thumb**
- Coincidence that *starts* trouble or reunites after long absence is usually forgivable
- Coincidence that *rescues* the climax reads as unearned
- Soft magic / external fix at climax → Aristotle: solution should arise from the plot

**Coincidence vs destiny framing**
- **Random world:** coincidences stay noise; characters may misread them as meaning (theme can be that misreading)
- **Pattern world (conspiracy, mythic, fate):** early “coincidences” must later re-read as design — seed early
- Prophecy/omen that removes choice flattens drama 

**Causality chain test**
1. For climax win/loss: list the last 5 causal links (A made B possible)
2. Any “and then luckily…” → replace with character choice, prior plant, or earlier cost
3. Try–fail: logistical delay ≠ psychological escalation; good fails make the *next* attempt harder
4. Audit soft-magic / outsider rescue against plant ledger 

**Allowed chance (sparingly)**
- Inciting accident that forces the story to start
- Near-miss that raises dread (bomb doesn’t explode *yet*)
- Social worlds where density makes meetings plausible — still stage motive
- Comedy piled coincidences as tonal contract 

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Climactic rescue by stranger / weather / forgotten sibling | Plant presence + motive earlier, or cut |
| Seventeen perfect conditions | Simplify; one prepared hinge |
| Detective lucky guess | Knox: declare clues; earn inference  |
| “It was all fate” with no choice cost | Restore dilemma  |

## Hard

**Aristotle / Horace bar**
- Solutions of plots should come about as a result of the plot itself, not from a contrivance (*Poetics*)
- Contrivance allowed for matters *outside* the drama (prior events beyond human knowledge, or later ones that need foretelling) — not as climax patch for a knot you wrote yourself into
- Horace: no god from the machine unless the difficulty is *worthy of a god’s unraveling* (`nec deus intersit, nisi dignus vindice nodus`)

**Deus ex diagnostics (symptom → cause)**
| Symptom | Cause | Move |
|---------|-------|------|
| Readers laugh / groan at rescue | Unprepared external fix | Plant agent + motive; or let failure stand |
| “How convenient” at HEA | Inheritance / long-lost relative / sudden cure | Seed in Act 1 as ordinary detail |
| Mystery solved by accident | Knox #6 breach | Earn inference from declared clues |
| Soft-magic win at climax | Sanderson-ish / Aristotle breach | Solution from established rules + character choice |

**When coincidence is *thematic tool***
- Wells *War of the Worlds*: bacteria kill Martians — often read as irony/theme (hubris, chance) not pure botch; still a hard sell in puzzle/fair-play contracts
- Golding called the navy officer in *Lord of the Flies* a “gimmick”; abrupt rescue can be deliberate tonal rupture — label it on purpose, don’t stumble into it
- Deliberate comic deus (e.g. absurd rescue) only under comedy/satire contract

**Prepared luck vs cheat**
- Prepared: ally was already in position because of earlier choice; skill trained on-page; world rule established
- Cheat: forgotten sibling, weather, passerby with perfect toolkit, detective hunch with no declared evidence
