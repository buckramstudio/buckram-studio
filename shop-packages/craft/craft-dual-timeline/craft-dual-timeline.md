# Dual-timeline craft

Braided timelines as novel structure — not a single flashback interrupt.

## Light

Job: run two dramatic spines that handshake until they collide.

Contract: past track is half the novel (or equal partner), not cayenne flashback.

Must-not-break:
- Each timeline has its own Want/clock and turns
- Every switch changes what the reader wants from the other track
- Convergence on purpose (event, revelation, or mirrored choice)
- Orientation convention (label / date / space break) kept consistent
- Don’t bait-and-switch the subject of the book with a false opening timeline

## Normal

**When dual timeline is the right tool**
- Past drives main plot via staged scenes with their own drama
- Secrets between characters / from self need reveal across both tracks
- Wrong tool when past is simple context — prefer linear weave / exposition drip

**Weiland technique family**
- Five backstory technique families include **alternating timelines** as distinct from prologue/flashback/linear weave
- Complex backstories suit simpler main plots; complex plots often need simpler backstories (or you drown)

**Braid design**
1. Two spines: Present Want/clock; Past Want/clock
2. Handshakes each switch (irony, dread, reclassification)
3. Information map: what reader knows that Present character doesn’t
4. Convergence beat designed, not hoped for
5. Length budget: long past episodes need full scene drama

**Orientation without lanterns**
- Avoid “Back in the present…” throat-clearing
- Drop time/place/POV clues in first lines; anchor to present detail when possible

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Past track is exposition dump | Give past its own GMC + turn |
| Reader abandons Present for strangers | Invest Present first; lean past casts |
| No convergence | Design collision beat |
| Flashback dressed as dual timeline | Shorten interrupt or commit to equal braid |
| Reveal dumps unplanted facts | Plant ledger  |

## Hard

**Flashback vs alternating timeline (decision)**
- Flashback: cayenne interrupt; short usually best; must move story inside the past *and* by placement
- Alternating timelines: equal dramatic partners; past not “memory slideshow”
- Opening on “wrong” timeline is a promise — hardest when it looks like flashback bait

**Irony map (Hard ops)**
- Track what Present character believes vs what Past scenes have already shown the reader
- Reclassification beats: a Past scene that rewrites a Present relationship is the form’s specialty — plant objects/scars early
- Domestic/unreliable flavors: pair with , 

**Failure pattern (named)**
- Long mid-story episode on barely introduced past cast → audience leaves the relationship they care about (Weiland/Friedman failure pattern) — keep past casts lean or invest Present first
