# Romance arc

Relationship growth as a through-line — external plot + each protagonist’s internal arc + couple engine. Market promises / heat / subgenre map: .

## Light

Job: keep the couple’s closeness/distance moving under plot pressure so HEA/HFN (or honest exit from category) is earned.

Contract (mass-market): plot revolves around developing romantic love; climax tied to that relationship; emotionally satisfying optimistic ending (HEA or HFN). Literary love stories may end tragically — that usually exits *category* romance.

Must-not-break:
- Meet → Attraction → Obstacles → Midpoint shift → Black Moment → Grand Gesture → HEA/HFN (skeleton)
- Yearning engine (tension ≠ mere conflict)
- Black moment: all seems lost *for the couple*
- Grand gesture earned by growth, not random fireworks
- Dual / alternating POV common — each internal arc visible

## Normal

**Why romance is harder to beat-sheet**
- Two (or more) protagonists, not one
- **External (plot) arc** + **internal arc per protagonist** + **Romance Arc** (relationship growth)
- Standard single-protagonist sheets bury the relationship through-line
- Companion approach: separate sheet focused on how plot beats affect the couple

**Genre-specific craft**
- Meet-cute (or meet-ugly) as charged first contact
- Dual / alternating POV common
- Black moment: all seems lost for the couple
- Tension ≠ conflict; **yearning** is the engine
- Grand gesture must be earned (setup through growth)
- Tropes index: Romance Tropes (TV Tropes) — use as catalog, not checklist theater

**Romance Planning Beat Sheet (concept)**
- Separates Romance Arc; same skeleton as a normal beat sheet
- Mixes Save the Cat + Story Engineering beats with Hauge Six Stage / Identity–Essence
- Question at each major beat: do obstacles **bring them together**, **break them apart**, or force **teamwork**?
- Target word count → page/word marks per beat (Excel tools on Jami Gold worksheets hub — do not hotlink file URLs)

**Shared beat names (plot sheet language)**
- **Inciting Incident / Catalyst** — life/game-changing event that puts the character on the story path
- **Pinch Points** (pre- and post-Midpoint) — more evidence of antagonistic forces; antidote to sagging middles
- **End of the Beginning** = Break into Act Two / First Plot Point — point-of-no-return choice; goals, stakes, obstacles
- **Midpoint** — second PONR; raise stakes via reversal, false reversal, or recommitment
- **Crisis** = Black Moment / All Is Lost / Second Plot Point — lose everything; symbolic death
- **Climax / Finale** — symbolic rebirth; courage/growth to overcome obstacles

Plot-only descriptions miss inner journey + romance arc — that’s why the romance-specific sheet exists.

**Identity → Essence (Hauge internal journey)**
Arc = journey from **living in fear** → **living courageously**; become who they have potential to be.

Identity stack (the mask / emotional armor):
1. **Longing / Need** — deep (often subconscious) unmet need
2. **Backstory Wound** — unhealed past pain coloring the world
3. **False Belief** — untrue-but-logical worldview from the wound
4. **Fear** — often fear of feeling that wound again

**Identity** = mask worn to stay safe. **Essence** = who they are / could become behind the armor; vulnerability required.

Plot forces the armor to stop fitting:
- ~Inciting: first cracks; coping mechanisms fail
- ~25% First Plot Point: conscious goal they can’t reach while armored; stubbornly keep the mask
- ~50% Midpoint: recognize they must change; experiment with mask off
- ~75% Crisis: catastrophic pain → full retreat into Identity
- Climax: stakes leave no choice; leap of faith into Essence → deserve to win

Outer journey = visible goal. Inner journey finish line = meet longing/need.

**Perfect-match romance mechanic**
- Perfect match **sees behind the armor** → conflict between who they think they are and who they can be
- Internal conflict: safe-but-unfulfilled ↔ terrified-but-fulfilled
- Scene rule of thumb:
 - **Closer / intimacy** after an emotional risk that shows Essence
 - **Fights / distance** after a retreat behind the mask
- Hint wound + Essence early so readers understand the mask; avoid hollow love-at-first-sight

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Plot beats, no couple motion | Ask together/apart/teamwork at each major beat |
| Black moment is plot-only | Make the loss *relational* |
| Grand gesture unearned | Seed growth; gesture proves Essence choice |
| Love-at-first-sight hollow | Hint wound + Essence early |
| Single-protagonist sheet | Track both internals + romance arc |

## Hard

**Dual Identity→Essence pairing**
- Map both protagonists’ Longing / Wound / Lie / Fear stacks
- Perfect-match pressure: each partner’s Essence threatens the other’s Identity safety
- Midpoint often: mutual glimpse of Essence *and* raised outer stakes
- Crisis: both retreat to Identity (or one abandons the other to armor) — couple black moment
- Climax: both leap (or one leap enables the other’s) — category HEA/HFN needs earned mutual vulnerability

**Category exit vs tragic love**
- RWA-style optimistic ending is a market promise, not a universal aesthetic law
- Tragic literary love can be excellent — relabel shelf expectations; don’t sell category romance then refuse HEA/HFN
- HFN vs HEA: series / contemporary often HFN; standalones often HEA — match series plan 

**When to break the skeleton**
- Slow-burn: stretch Attraction/Obstacles; still need midpoint shift and black moment functions
- Second-chance / marriage-in-trouble: “meet” may be re-meet; charged contact still required
- Ensemble / poly / RH: romance arc may braid multiple dyads — each needs together/apart pressure ( et al. for market engines)
