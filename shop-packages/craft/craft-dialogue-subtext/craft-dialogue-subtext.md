# Dialogue and subtext

Dramatic dialogue as verbal action — not transcribed small talk.

## Light

Job: make each line *do* something to someone (pressure, seduce, wound, escape, extract).

Contract: subtext = solvable gap between Said and Unsaid/Unsayable — not opaque mutter.

Must-not-break:
- Pre-write: “In this scene, X wants ____ from Y.”
- Diagnostic: “What is this line trying to **do**?”
- Said ≡ unsaid → on-the-nose (zero subtext)
- Incompatible wants → duel (usually stronger than a duet)
- Value charge should flip at least once or the talk is decoration

## Normal

**Dialogue is action**
- Each character enters with a want for this exchange; dialogue = tactics
- Beat = action/reaction; label lines with present-tense verbs (baiting, deflecting…); same verb twice → likely redundant

**Three spheres**
- **Said:** words spoken (only sphere you write)
- **Unsaid:** conscious feelings deliberately withheld
- **Unsayable:** subconscious wound/need the character can’t name
- Write a surface inadequate to the emotional pressure; reader fills the gap
- Gap must be solvable (context, characterization, physical cue)
- Novel trap: constant interiority closes every gap; let dialogue carry subtext first

**Grice maxims — flout on purpose**

Cooperative conversation expects Quantity / Quality / Relation / Manner. Drama flouts them:

| Maxim | Flout produces | Example move |
|-------|----------------|--------------|
| Quantity | too little or too much | one-word answer to a huge question; rehearsed flood |
| Quality | irony, lies, sarcasm | “This is fine” amid disaster |
| Relation | topic swerve | “Do you still love him?” → “The kettle’s boiling.” |
| Manner | register shift | blunt speaker goes suddenly lawyerly |

- Don’t flout every line — cooperative speech around the flout makes it land
- Answering a question that wasn’t asked reveals what they fear being asked

**On-the-nose failure modes**
- Fear reader won’t get it → announce emotions, dump backstory, confirm shared knowledge
- Fix: incompatible objectives; neither names the want; talk around it
- Direct confession hits hardest when surrounded by earned subtext

**Voice habits**
- Voice = what they notice, avoid, reach for under pressure — not only slang
- Distinct avoidance: weather, humor, silence, wrong-question answers
- Silence / unfinished sentences / object business are dialogue
- Avoid tags that label emotion; action beats show the gap
- After ~3 speech chunks, break with beat, speaker switch, or flash of thought

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Friendly chit-chat with no want | Give each speaker a tactical goal |
| Interiority narrates every gap | Cut thought; leave the line |
| Opaque mutter | Add solvable cue / context |
| Same deflection verb forever | Vary tactics; flip value charge |

## Hard

**Grice (Paul Grice, “Logic and Conversation,” 1975) — precise bar**
- Cooperative Principle: make your contribution such as is required by the accepted purpose of the talk exchange
- Maxims of Quantity (informative as required, not more), Quality (truth), Relation (relevance), Manner (clear, orderly, brief, unambiguous)
- **Implicature:** meaning conveyed by *flouting* a maxim while still being assumed cooperative — subtext is often implicature the reader/listener recovers
- **Flout vs violate:** flout = listener is meant to recover the real meaning (sarcasm, kettle swerve); violate = deceive (lie, hide the abandoned station) — mystery/villain speech often *violates*; intimate subtext usually *flouts*
- Craft mapping: on-the-nose speech over-obeys Quantity/Manner; drama under-informs, misdirects, swerves, or goes oddly precise

**Status / intimacy coupling**
- Flouts are also status moves  — who may change the subject, who must answer
- Isolation revision: read each speaker’s lines alone — if they always say what they want, flatten 

**When to speak plain**
- Climactic confession / contract scenes earn directness after a book of flouts — don’t withhold the Said forever
- Kidlit / some comedy contracts allow more on-the-nose; still keep wants incompatible
