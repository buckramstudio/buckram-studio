# Antihero craft

Sympathy and agency for morally illegible leads — without collapsing into cartoon villain or secretly-pure hero.

## Light

Job: keep a compromised protagonist readable as the experiential center.

Contract: reader endures methods they wouldn’t endorse — needs a tether, not a secret sainthood reveal.

Must-not-break:
- Clear Want (even ugly) > mushy nihilism
- Show harm to others *and* to self (cost visibility)
- A line they won’t cross (moving line = arc; no line = monster tourism)
- Choices under pressure — not “the darkness made me”
- Endings chosen on purpose: punishment / pyrrhic / change / thematic refusal

## Normal

**What it is (craft)**
- Protagonist who pursues goals with methods/ethics the audience wouldn’t endorse — yet remains the story’s center
- Not “villain POV” alone; not “flawed hero” with a cute vice
- Focal conflict often engaged by personal will more than calling to greater good; personal motives first
- May perform heroic acts while remaining morally ambiguous

**Sympathy levers**
| Lever | Use |
|-------|-----|
| Clear Want | Readable drive |
| Competence | Skill ≠ moral approval |
| Cost visibility | Harm invoiced |
| Line they won’t cross | Scale + arc |
| Lie / Need | Still has a Lie; Truth may be bitter |
| Contrast cast | Foil better/worse clarifies scale |

**Agency**
- Prefer dilemmas with two costly goods over cartoon evil monologues 
- Humor/charm can buy page-time; don’t let charm erase consequence

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Edgy without tether | Add code / relationship / wit with limits |
| Secret cinnamon-roll cheat | Commit or rebrand as flawed hero |
| Harm without bill | Invoice in plot and relationships |
| Villain collapse (roots against them) | Restore Want + tether + competence |

## Hard

**Named lineages (use as lenses, not costumes)**
- Classical / satirical / picaresque roots (Thersites; Don Quixote; rogue tradition)
- **Byronic** and Gothic double strands (Romantic antihero)
- **Racinian** pattern (scholarly): doomed to fail; blames others; critiques social morals
- Existential mid-century: drift, angst, alienation (Kafka / Sartre / Camus cluster)
- American mid-century: alienated, often more proactive than French counterparts; “angry young men” British strand
- Contemporary prestige TV antihero / antiheroine boom — complex women who subvert nurturing/compliance expectations (scholarship cluster on wiki)

**Viewpoint craft edges**
- Antihero can be protagonist *or* antagonistic force; still the conflict’s engine
- “Hero from one point of view, villain from another” — dual moral framing is a feature; pick whose experiential center the reader rides
- Northrop Frye note (via wiki): shift of heroic ethos / center of gravity — epic virtue → ironic narrative; don’t force feudal-hero beats onto ironic leads

**Ending / theme diagnostics**
| Ending type | Thematic bill |
|-------------|----------------|
| Punishment | Moral order reasserts — can feel tidy or earned |
| Pyrrhic win | Want achieved; Need unpaid |
| Earned change | Lie released at cost |
| Refusal / flat darkness | Theme must argue why refusal matters |

Shelf flavors (grimdark / noir / dark fantasy) keep engines in genre files; reuse this for any shelf.
