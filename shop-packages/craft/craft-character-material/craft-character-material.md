# Character material

Trait lists, templates, and character-craft notes. Builder fill-ins: . Wound/arc deepener: , .

## Light

Job: turn trait inventories into on-page pressure — beliefs that can crash, flaws that force hard choices.

Contract: mains need convictions that get challenged; sides can stay rough.

Must-not-break:
- Want (immediate + permanent) before wardrobe trivia
- Negative/annoying traits that create ally friction — not cartoon villainy
- Fatal flaw = situation the climax must force them to face
- Prefer show via action/dialogue/interaction over trait essays
- Backstory at peak decision pressure > early dump
- Typology (MBTI / Enneagram) = brainstorm only, not destiny dialogue

## Normal

**Quick attribute checklist**
- where they live what they wear treasured object
- something they love / hate / would never do
- unique skill weird habit
- what they want (immediately and permanently)

**Scene tracker (who/what/when/where/why/how)**
- who is IN this scene what they do that impacts action when they show up where exactly why here how appearance impacts others

**Static vs dynamic**
- **Static:** unchanged; useful as opposition force; story isn’t “about” them
- **Dynamic:** forced to choose differently; backstory explains why *this* choice is hard
- Easy in-character choices = static; drama needs identity threat + difficult choice
- Prefer one self-contained flashback story over multi-chapter history when experience motivates a decision

**Roles (compact)**
- Protagonist — central figure we follow; often flawed
- Antagonist — opposing goals (not always “evil”); believable motives
- Deuteragonist — second lead / ally; own growth that parallels or contrasts

**Direct vs indirect characterization**
- **Direct:** narrator/character states qualities
- **Indirect:** infer from actions, speech, appearance, mannerisms, others’ reactions
- Voice: diction/cadence should identify speakers without tags alone 

**Typology lenses (brainstorm only)**
- MBTI-style: habits of attention — spark, not destiny
- Enneagram: core fear / desire / stress shifts — stronger for internal conflict
- Prefer: one wound + one contradiction + one coping strategy over questionnaire dump
- Jung / brand-12 → 

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Trait list, no pressure | Crash two beliefs; force against conviction |
| Early wound dump | Move reveal to peak decision |
| Antagonist explained too early | Withhold; keep protagonist agency |
| Type-description dialogue | Cut; write contradiction instead |
| Perfect hero | Add friction traits allies notice |

**Personality sketcher (tool-ready)**
- Input: rich description → output: organized third-person sketch; dispassionate; suitable as AI context

## Hard

**Plot primacy vs character primacy (durable debate)**
- Aristotle (*Poetics*): tragedy as representation of action and life — plot over character portraits
- 19th-century realist + psychological novel reversed the prestige toward character-driven narrative
- Drafting use: if the book stalls in biography, restore action pressure; if action is empty spectacle, restore belief-crash choices

**Archetype pitfall**
- Jungian / brand-12 lists help brainstorm; “flat” characters that never leave one archetype read as cliché
- Complex characters combine pressures; shadow/mentor/trickster as *functions* > costume labels 

**Voice as characterization**
- Age, culture, education, formality, circumstance shape speech — mannerisms help but are not the whole person
- Reader should often identify the speaker from how they talk alone
