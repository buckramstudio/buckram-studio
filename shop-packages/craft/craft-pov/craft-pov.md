## Light

Job: choose who sees, how close, and whether credibility itself is the game — on purpose.

Contract: a controlling narrator (or clear single-head limit) beats accidental head-hops.

Must-not-break:
- Choose POV intentionally; contemporary default craving is close third / first
- Omniscient needs a conductor voice — access without control = head-hopping
- Limit which heads get access
- Unreliable = compromised credibility vs the work’s norms (Booth)
- Fallible ≠ untrustworthy (Olson) — different reader responses
- Multi-POV conflicting witnesses ≠ one clown narrator

## Normal

**Omniscient — intentional, not default**
- Case for: bird’s-eye multi-thread; narrator as distinct entity; dramatic irony
- Case against: distance vs immersion craving; easier to tell than show; head-hop risk
- **Omniscient:** a conductor — access to many minds, someone clearly in control
- **Head-hopping:** no conductor — POV flips without warning
- Best practices: decide who the narrator is and what they sound like; keep that voice consistent; limit which heads get access; if feedback hates omniscient, test deep third/first

**Unreliable narration — definitions that matter**
- **Booth** (*Rhetoric of Fiction*, 1961): unreliable when speech/acts don’t accord with norms of the work (implied author)
- **Rabinowitz**: unreliability = lies/concealment/misjudgment relative to the *narrative audience’s* standards
- **Olson**: **fallible** (wrong about facts/interpretation) vs **untrustworthy** (deliberately misleading)
- **Phelan**: bonding unreliability can draw authorial audience closer vs estranging
- **Wall** on *Remains of the Day*: reliable fact-channel vs unreliable judgment-channel

**Riggan types (first-person focus)**

| Type | Engine |
|------|--------|
| **Pícaro** | Entertaining rogue; self-serving morality gap |
| **Clown** | Irony, ambiguity, reversals |
| **Madman** | Unbalanced mind; perversely logical |
| **Naïf** | Lacks experience; reveals without awareness |

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Head-hop without conductor | Name narrator stance or stay in one head |
| One weird claim = “unreliable” | Pattern across signal channels |
| Pure rug-pull twist | Fair clues; solid fact floor |
| Omniscient by accident | Choose close third/first or commit to narrator |

## Hard

**Reveal timing**
- **Immediate** — opening delusion, admitted illness, frame that tags the teller
- **Delayed twist** — end forces total reread; high risk of cheat if clues weren’t fair
- **Never settled** — interpretive tension; hints only

**Signals (Nünning-style clusters)**
1. **Intratextual** — self-contradiction, memory gaps, lies, mismatched affect
2. **Extratextual** — clashes with world-knowledge or logical impossibility (within story physics)
3. **Literary competence** — genre/stock-type cues that train suspicion
- Plant at least two channels; one weird claim can read as worldbuilding

**Design checklist**
- What does the narrator *want* the narrative audience to believe?
- Fallible or untrustworthy (or sliding)?
- Which facts are solid so the reader has a floor?
- When does the implied author want bonding vs estrangement?
- Does the climax pay the unreliability without a pure rug-pull?
- Multi-POV / Rashomon: conflicting reliable-enough witnesses ≠ one clown narrator
- Psychic distance / FID can *show* self-deception without confessional first person
