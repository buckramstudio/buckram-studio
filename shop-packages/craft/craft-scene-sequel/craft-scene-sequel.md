# Scene and sequel (Swain / Ingermanson)

Large-scale scene structure: proactive Scene vs reactive Sequel, alternating.

**Builder (Scene/Sequel cards):** hub 

## Light

Job: keep the story moving by alternating pursuit (Scene) with recovery-and-choice (Sequel).

Contract: after a real setback, the character feels, weighs bad options, then decides — that Decision becomes the next Goal.

Must-not-break:
- Scene Goal is specific and measurable at scene start
- Conflict escalates; easy victory is boring
- Disaster = propelling outcome (not always catastrophe)
- Sequel needs Reaction before a new Goal Scene
- Decision must be believable from emotion + thought
- Structure serves emotional experience, not paint-by-numbers

## Normal

**Two patterns**
- **Scene** (proactive): Goal → Conflict → Disaster
- **Sequel** (reactive): Reaction → Dilemma → Decision
- Ordinary “scene” in a novel may be either; Swain capitalized Scene/Sequel to distinguish
- Reader motivation = powerful emotional experience

**Scene: Goal–Conflict–Disaster**
- **Goal:** POV wants something specific; proactive; reader can score progress
- **Conflict:** obstacles on the way; escalate; direct or indirect opposition
- **Disaster:** setback / failure / “yes, but” / “no, and…” — worsens the situation so the page turns
- Hang the character off a cliff at scene end; “Disaster” means *propelling outcome*, not always catastrophe

**Sequel: Reaction–Dilemma–Decision**
- After a real Disaster you cannot instantly open a new Goal Scene
- **Reaction:** visceral emotional follow-through; time may pass
- **Dilemma:** no good options (or least-bad only); reader worries
- **Decision:** choose a path → becomes Goal of the next Scene
- Decision restores proactivity; people who never decide are boring on the page

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Easy win | Add cost / “yes, but” |
| Instant next Goal after gut-punch | Write or imply Sequel |
| Vague Goal | Make it scoreable |
| Dilemma with obvious best choice | Close the easy exits |
| Decision from nowhere | Show emotion → thought → choice |

## Hard

**Multi-POV / compressed sequels**
- Rigid Scene→Sequel→Scene forever traps you in one head
- Modern novels often play Sequels off-camera; Decision meat appears inside another character’s Scene
- Author still must *know* every Sequel; reader gets high points without a full reaction chapter
- One POV’s Sequel can be dramatized as another POV’s Scene

**Create then impose**
- Draft freely first; edit later to impose Scene/Sequel (+ MRU) structure
- Patterns within patterns: Scenes/Sequels are sequences of MRUs — don’t starve or drag either layer

**When to break the rigid alternation**
- Compressed Sequel (one paragraph of reaction + decision) after a mild setback
- Sequel-as-montage across time jumps
- Never skip *knowing* the Sequel psychology even when the page compresses it
