# Story Grid — five commandments & genre as obligation

Shawn Coyne’s Story Grid: nested units that each shift a **value**, plus genre as a contract of **conventions** and **obligatory moments**. Distill for diagnosis — not a substitute for Coyne’s full toolkit.

## Light

Job: diagnose whether a scene/story unit actually turns a value — and whether genre obligations land on the page.

Contract: fail the genre contract → “wrong book” feeling even if prose is fine; fail the five → busy units that don’t change anything.

Must-not-break:
- Every working scene can show the five (not always equally loud)
- Inciting incident must promise / pair with climax
- Crisis is a real binary dilemma (Best Bad Choice or Irreconcilable Goods)
- Conventions ≠ obligatory moments
- Global-genre obligations belong **on the page**, not offstage

## Normal

**Core idea**
- Story units (beat → scene → sequence → act → global) each move a **life value** (life/death, love/hate, freedom/slavery…)
- Genre = audience expectation package: what *must* appear (conventions) + which payoff events must land (obligatory moments)

**Five Commandments (fractal)**

| # | Commandment | Job |
|---|-------------|-----|
| 1 | **Inciting Incident** | Destabilizes balance (causal or coincidental); goal forms |
| 2 | **Turning Point Progressive Complication** | Strategies fail until a final turn (opposition or revelation) makes old approach useless |
| 3 | **Crisis** | Binary dilemma — Best Bad Choice *or* Irreconcilable Goods |
| 4 | **Climax** | Active answer to the crisis; reveals who they are under pressure |
| 5 | **Resolution** | Meaningful consequence; stakes must pay |

**Crisis types**
- **Best Bad Choice** — two terrible options; either costs
- **Irreconcilable Goods** — two goods; choosing one forgoes the other

**Conventions vs obligatory moments**

| Term | What it is | Failure mode |
|------|------------|--------------|
| **Conventions** | Roles, settings, circumstances, catalysts | “Is this even X?” |
| **Obligatory moments** | Key events/decisions/revelations that pay expectations | Cheated even if “surprise” |

**Obligatory-moment flavors**
1. Unexpected events — opposing goals create responsive conflict
2. Revelations — new info or new view of old info forces strategy change
3. Decisions and actions — climax-type agency that changes self/world

**Gilbo build-your-own list**
1. Pick 5–10 **comp titles** in your global genre
2. Shared roles/settings/circumstances → conventions
3. Shared must-see events → obligatory scenes
4. Put **global-genre** obligations on the page (subplot obligations may be thinner)

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Scene busy, value flat | Find crisis + climax of the unit |
| Inciting unresolved at end | Pair II ↔ climax promise |
| Random turning point | Make it failure of hero’s strategy |
| Genre “feels wrong” | Audit conventions + on-page obligations |

## Hard

**Common commandment mistakes**
- Inciting incident doesn’t promise / pair with the climax
- Inciting left unresolved at story end
- Turning point is a random drop-in, not failure of the hero’s strategy
- Weak link from turning point → forced crisis
- Different character faces crisis than enacts climax
- Climax “works” with no cost — resolution ignores crisis stakes

- Distill for diagnosis — not a substitute for Coyne’s full spreadsheet toolkit
- Subplot obligations may stay thinner; **global** genre obligations should not
- Literary/low-obligation shelves still need value shifts if they claim dramatic change
