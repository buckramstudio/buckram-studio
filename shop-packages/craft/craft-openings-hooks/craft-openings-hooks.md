# Openings and hooks

First page / Chapter 1 craft: promise, motion, question — not information dump.

**Canonical for book openings.** Ongoing chapter endings/hooks (any chapter): — related pair, not a duplicate.

Kidlit formats: (page-turn opens), , (fast voice+problem).

## Light

Job: promise kind of story, who we follow, world, and a question that pulls forward — in motion.

Contract: create hunger before explanation; start in scene, not briefing.

Must-not-break:
- Specific promise → momentum; vague promise → put-down
- Protagonist present and particular
- Only enough info for *this* scene’s contours
- Feel something before fully understanding
- Soft rule: don’t open on waking up
- Avoid cheap rug-pulls and vagueness-as-mystery

## Normal

**Contract of the opening**
Promise four things: kind of story, who we follow, world we’re in, question that pulls forward.

**Start with scene, not briefing**
- Don’t begin from “info the reader needs for the plot”
- Ask: awesome scene? evocative setting with protagonist in element? thorny situation? active from line one?
- Pre-inciting “what is” OK (Shire / Tatooine) if character stays active — mini-quest before main quest
- Draft can use a placeholder opening; rewrite showstopper after you know the book

**First-page diagnostics**
- First sentence creates a question?
- When does the *real* story begin on this page?
- Backstory in first ~250 words — essential or nervous padding?
- Whose head? Five sensory details filtered through *their* attention
- Emotional situation *now* (state, not history)

**First-chapter elements**
- Place woven into action (long static description openings kill)
- Conflict or foreshadowed imbalance
- Narrative voice established
- Curiosity hook / open loop
- Tension ≠ always explosion; something should be off

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Info before investment | Cut to active scene |
| Sock-puppet exposition | Imply via conflict |
| Wake-up open | Start on next meaningful action |
| Setup delaying to page 3 | Move real story earlier |

## Hard

**Hook patterns (mechanics)**
1. **Competence + terminal stakes** — expert at work; casually this is the last time
2. **Slightly wrong ordinary** — detailed normal; one denial-able anomaly; dramatic irony
3. **Return to forbidden place** — body language carries backstory
4. **Dialogue in medias res** — argue as if reader already knows
5. **Numerical stakes** — count + math deficit; world via constraint
6. **Suppression** — catastrophic news; character refuses to react yet
7. **Expert vs impossible** — credentials make alarm credible
8. **List as story** — packing/inventory juxtapositions without commentary
9. **Lie after truth** — reader saw blood-wash; now watches perfect alibi
10. **Destination first** — open with ending fact; suspense is the approach

Shared anti-pattern: explain before the reader hungers for explanation.

**Loose 3-page test**
- ~Page 1: urgent narrative question
- ~Page 2: grounding (who/where/texture)
- ~Page 3: stakes felt — Want, threatened Need, why *this* person
