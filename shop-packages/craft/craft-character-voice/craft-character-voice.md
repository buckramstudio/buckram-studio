# Character voice

Keep cast members from sharing one formal default voice — each speaker should be identifiable by how they talk.

## Light

Job: make dialogue and interiority carry identity so tags become optional.

Contract: cover the names — a reader can still tell who is speaking from rhythm, diction, and attitude.

Must-not-break:
- Distinct emotional default per major character (not shared “eloquent mild”)
- At least two speech quirks that show on the page (length, fillers, formality…)
- Allowed / never words differ across the cast
- Blind spots and wrongness allowed — competence/EQ is not universal
- Same stimulus → different reaction patterns

## Normal

**Failure tells (undifferentiated cast)**
- Villain, love interest, and gruff mercenary all sound eloquent and slightly formal
- Everyone “muses” / “ponders” / speaks in complete sentences
- Personality essays ≠ felt voice; pattern-match to tropes
- Same meaning, different people should diverge: teen “that’s literally so wrong” vs professor “I’m not certain that follows”

**Six ingredients (ratios must differ)**
1. **Emotional default** — state at rest (curious, anxious, humorous…). Chief driver of the rest
2. **Diction** — word choices
3. **Syntax** — word order / sentence shape
4. **Rhythm & pace** — speed, urgency, hesitancy
5. **Formality register** — formal ↔ informal
6. **Catchphrases & tics** — repeats under stress (use sparingly)

Backstory (class, education, culture, hobbies, wound) shapes emotional default → shapes the six. No two characters share the same ratios.

**Dialogue samples over trait essays**
Prefer 3–5 example lines that show rhythm/word choice/attitude over “Marcus is gruff…”

Example pattern:
- Short, clipped; interrupts
- “Yeah. And?”
- “Don’t care. Moving on.”
- “You finished? Good. Here’s what’s actually happening.”

**Speech quirks (pick ~2 per character)**
- Sentence length: fragments vs long windings
- Fillers: Look / Listen / I mean / Right?
- Questions vs statements (asks permission vs only tells)
- Formality: cannot vs can’t
- Vocabulary range: simple vs fancy

**Ban shared vocabulary**
- Per-character allowed / never words (Lena: beautiful/gentle; Marcus: fine/solid)
- Global blacklist for overused session words so alternatives get more specific

**Same stimulus, different reaction**
Test: two characters get same bad news — quiet vs loud vs joke vs blame.
- Mira: deflects with humor, changes subject, avoids eye contact
- Jonas: very still, speaks slower, asks clarifying questions

**Let characters be wrong**
Default competence/EQ bleeds voices together. Specify blind spots:
- Takes jokes literally / bad at social cues
- Assumes worst; hears insults in neutral lines

**Per-character voice card (minimal)**
- 3–5 example dialogue lines
- Two speech quirks
- Words they use / never use
- Stress / conflict reaction
- What they get wrong

**Test:** cover names — can you tell who’s speaking from how they talk?

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Need a tag every two lines | Push diction + syntax apart; change emotional defaults |
| Everyone eloquent | Cap vocabulary; allow fragments / interruptions |
| Trait essay, flat dialogue | Replace essay with sample lines + quirks |
| Catchphrase spam | One tic under stress; cut the rest |
| Voice vanishes in sex/fight | Keep register; don’t swap to thesaurus voice |

**Case (*Six of Crows*):** Kaz = cynical, transactional diction, punchy fragments, fast, slang; Inej = quietly hopeful, sensory diction, complete/proverbial sentences, pauses, Suli saints/proverbs — similar trauma, opposite defaults → distinct voices.

## Hard

**Idiolect (linguistics)**
- An individual’s unique language use: active vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation — not the same as dialect (group-shared features)
- Every speaker has one, shaped by language community, socioeconomic status, geography
- Forensic linguistics treats idiolect as authorship fingerprint (style consistency can identify or rule out a writer)
- Craft implication: aim for a stable private dialect per major character; dialect notes go in , idiolect is the personal layer on top

**Writers who “hear” characters (research note)**
Durham / Guardian / Edinburgh Book Festival survey (n=181 festival authors; Consciousness and Cognition):
- ~63% hear characters speak while writing; ~61% characters can act independently
- ~56% visual/other sensory experience of characters; ~20% sense shared physical space; ~15% dialogue with creations
- Not hallucination pathology; vivid imagination / personality models like real-people prediction
- Some authors control which series-voices are active; some experience subconscious problem-solving rather than “possession”
- Use: if a voice feels “wrong,” treat it as model conflict — update the card, don’t force author eloquence onto the character

**One character, one agent (optional ops)**
Multi-character single AI → voice bleed. Dedicated instance per major character (plus narrator) improves consistency for long-form.

**Anonymous-dialogue diagnostic**
1. Strip tags and action beats from a scene
2. Label speakers by voice only
3. Below ~80% accuracy → contamination; change emotional default first, then syntax length, then diction

**When to break “distinct voice”**
- Chorus / mob / ritual speech may converge on purpose
- Mimicry, code-switching, or performance of another’s register is character data — mark the return to default
- Intimate pairs may share private lexicon without becoming interchangeable
