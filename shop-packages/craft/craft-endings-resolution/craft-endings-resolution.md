## Light

Job: end the conflict through earned choice, then give aftermath space to feel cost and change.

Contract: Climactic Moment ends the pursuit of the plot goal; Resolution is emotional mop-up, not more plot.

Must-not-break:
- Protagonist drives resolution (allies help; unearned rescue steals meaning)
- Arc completes in action under pressure
- Pay off major setups; cost of victory usually feels truer
- Don’t collapse climax + resolution + denouement into one rushed chapter
- Resolve as many subplots as possible *before* Climax
- Genre promises still bind (HEA/HFN, fair mystery solution, etc.)

## Normal

**Three-part ending (practical)**
1. **Climax** — central conflict resolves through confrontation/choice; stakes max; protagonist active
2. **Resolution** — immediate aftermath; consequences; answer the central story question (often ~2–5% of book)
3. **Denouement / new normal** — brief glimpse of changed life; final emotional note

**Climax vs Climactic Moment (Weiland)**
- **Climax** ≈ final ~eighth (from ~88%): sequence funneling toward end of conflict
- **Climactic Moment** = exact instant the conflict *ends* (win, permanent loss, or deliberate relinquishing)
- Pursuit of the plot goal stops here
- **Faux climax** — characters think conflict ended; true obstacle remains — does not replace the real Climactic Moment

**Earned endings**
- Pay off setups; arc completes in action; setup the tools of victory earlier
- Avoid unearned deus ex; invoice cost of victory
- Inevitability > surprise: “of course” in retrospect; surprise lives in *how* and *cost*

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Rushed climax after long buildup | Give confrontation room |
| Passive protagonist / crane rescue | Agency + foreshadowed tools |
| Too neat / too messy | Cost + major threads land |
| Long “what everyone did next” | Lean Resolution; hint lives continue |
| Epilogue explains everything | Fix in Resolution instead |

## Hard

**Resolution duties (Weiland)**
- Begins right after Climax; shorter is generally better
- Tie prominent loose ends without being too pat
- Preview new life after conflict; hint lives continue (standalone *and* series)
- Dramatize how the journey changed people
- Strike final emotional tone that matches the book
- Structural test: Hook → Inciting → major beats should thematically align with Climactic Moment

**Open vs closed**
- **Closed** — major plot question answered; conflict ended; new status clear
- **Open / series handoff** — this book’s conflict still ends; Resolution may point to next pursuit
- Ambiguity OK for philosophical residue or minor threads; frustrating if the *tracked* main question never answers

**Ending checklist**
- List every major promise/thread from early chapters — each addressed?
- Does Climactic Moment require the growth the middle earned?
- Aftermath space enough to feel cost and change?
- Final image/line — resonant, ideally echoes opening transformed?
- Tone matches what the book taught readers to expect?
