# Narrative devices (prose craft)

Chekhov’s gun, foreshadowing, deus ex, unreliable narration, stream of consciousness. Related: , , , .

## Light

Job: name the classic devices so you plant, pay off, and avoid unearned rescues on purpose.

Contract: introduce only what the story will use (or deliberately leave as life-noise); solutions arise from the plot.

Must-not-break:
- Chekhov economy: don’t hang a rifle that never matters (unless side-shadowing on purpose)
- Foreshadowing ≠ red herring ≠ flashforward
- Deus ex usually damages belief; prepare tools of victory earlier
- Unreliable = compromised credibility relative to the work’s norms
- Stream of consciousness costs clarity/pace — pair with scene goals in commercial work

## Normal

**Chekhov’s gun**
- Original intent (conservation): every element necessary; remove irrelevancies
- Modern drift: also “plant early, payoff later” — related but distinct from foreshadowing / MacGuffin / red herring

**Foreshadowing**
- Hints/warnings of events to come — dialogue, events, setting, titles
- Builds suspense; later “bizarre” events feel earned

**Deus ex machina**
- Abrupt external fix for an unsolvable knot (“god from the machine”)
- Aristotle: solutions should arise from the plot itself
- Usually damages logic; sometimes deliberate comedy or thematic astonishment

**Unreliable narrator**

**Stream of consciousness**
- Multitudinous thoughts/feelings — often disjointed, free association
- Vs interior monologue: SoC mingles impressions; IM can stay ordered
- Craft use: intimacy; cost = clarity/pace

## Hard

**Named debates / spectra**
- **Chekhov pushback:** Hemingway valued inconsequential detail; Phillips argues single-role details make stories predictable; Chekhov left unfired guns in *The Cherry Orchard* thematically
- **Sideshadowing (Morson):** scenes that stay inconsequential, like life, to raise verisimilitude — opposite of gun-must-fire
- **Unreliable spectra (Olson):** fallible (wrong about facts/interpretation) vs untrustworthy (deliberately misleading) — different reader responses
- **Reveal timing:** immediate (delusion/frame) delayed twist never fully settled
- **Signals (Nünning-ish):** intratextual contradiction/gaps extratextual impossibility genre/competence cues

**When to break “gun must fire”**
- Thematic residue / atmosphere as the point of the detail
- Deliberate life-noise (sideshadowing) in literary modes
- Red herring that *looks* like a gun — then reassign payoff honestly
