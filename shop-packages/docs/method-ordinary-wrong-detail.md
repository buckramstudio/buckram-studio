# Ordinary to wrong detail

## What this method changes

Ground the reader in an ordinary, legible situation, then alter the scene's meaning with one concrete anomaly reported in the same calm register as everything around it.

**Narrator priority:** Orientation first, anomaly second, reaction third. The narrator notices practical surfaces and social behavior before interpreting significance.

## Best fit

Close-third or lightly voiced omniscient narration; discovery, social unease, domestic wonder, and scenes where the extraordinary should feel embedded in daily life rather than announced.

## Hard limits

**Anti-caricature:** Not constant whimsical oddity. ≥half selected detail ordinary/functional; wry capped at one brief turn per paragraph, usually rarer.

- Do not label strange/impossible/magical/ominous/significant before evidence.
- Do not stack >1 new anomaly per beat.
- Do not use rhetorical questions to amaze.
- Do not turn wryness into quips/punchlines/collusion.
