# Singability

Job: lines a mouth can sing on a first pass. Not prose with line breaks.

## Light

Must-not-break:
- About **6–10 words per line** (speech stress). Cut the 14-word clause.
- Stress lands where a speaker would hit it. Do not bury the title on a weak syllable.
- Chorus holds want **open vowels**: oh, ay, I, you, ah. Closed clusters (`strict`, `glimpse`) belong in verses if anywhere.
- **Perfect rhyme on chorus landings.** Verses may slant.
- One idea per line. Nested asides fail.

## Normal

### Stress check

Speak the line out loud (or subvocalize). If you need a comma mid-line to survive, split the line.

Bad (prose):
`I was thinking about the way you left the porch light on for nobody`

Better:
`You left the porch light on`
`for nobody`

### Chorus vs verse density

| | Chorus | Verse |
|--|--------|-------|
| Words/line | fewer | still ~6–10, more nouns |
| Rhyme | perfect on landings | slant OK |
| Images | 1 repeating | new each verse |
| Function | remember | evidence |

### Holds

Put the title or the emotional noun on the hold, not the filler (`just`, `really`, `that I`).

### Internal rhyme / stacks

Allowed in hip-hop and some pop pre-choruses. Forbidden as a default in blues, folk, torch — those need air.

## Hard

Do not count syllables as a religion. Count **stresses** and breath.

Do not write a chorus denser than verse 1 “because the hook should be clever.” Clever is verse work. Chorus is catch.
