# Hook and title

Job: the title **is** the promise. The hook is that promise made singable and slightly unfinished.

## Light

Must-not-break:
- One title. It appears in the chorus (usually the landing line).
- Hook is repeatable in one breath.
- Hook is **slightly incomplete** — the verses finish the picture; the hook does not summarize the novel.
- Do not hide the title only in verse 2.

## Normal

### Title tests

1. Can you say it to a friend as the whole idea? If not, it is a mood, not a title.
2. Does the chorus need the title to land? If you can delete it and the chorus still works, the title is decoration.
3. Is it short enough to shout / hum? Long clauses fail on the hold.

### Hook jobs

| Hook type | What it does | Trap |
|-----------|--------------|------|
| Promise | “I won’t call.” | Explaining why in the same line |
| Image | “Blue kitchen light.” | Stacking three images |
| Chant | “Hands up — wait.” | Writing a paragraph chant |
| Question | “Who’s at the door?” | Answering in the hook |
| Moral (country) | Title restates the small plot’s lesson | Fortune-cookie abstraction |

### Chorus vs verses

- Chorus: fewer words, more open vowels, same lines each time (or one last-chorus twist max).
- Verses: specific names, places, objects. They earn the right to repeat the hook.

## Hard

Do not write two hooks and “see which one the model likes.” Lock one.

Do not put the title in every line. Once per chorus cycle is enough unless the palette is a chant (anthem, shanty, K-pop tag).
