# Pirate / nautical adventure

Engine: ship as society; sea as engine (weather, navigation, scarcity); command, mutiny, prize, and code under pressure; horizontal world of decks and vertical world of rank.

## Light

Job: immerse the reader in nautical culture under voyage pressure — sea tests the sailor; ship hierarchy is the social plot.

Contract: conventional sea story = embark → tested by sea / colleagues / shore → made or broken; readers expect adventure, maritime accuracy texture, and nautical language as seasoning.

Must-not-break:
- Ship geography clear (or deliberately labyrinthine for newcomers)
- Chain of command and its breaking points
- Sea set-pieces with resource math (storm, chase, boarding, becalming)
- Port episodes as pressure valves and temptation
- Enough jargon for texture, not exam
- Avoid theme-park pirates (talk-like-a without economy of violence)

## Normal

**Genre shape**

- Nautical / naval / sea fiction: setting on or near the sea; human relationship to sea voyages; merchant, naval, fishing, liners, ports
- Pirate lane: outlaw maritime predation / temporary republic; romance and grimdark variants
- Lineage anchors: Cooper *The Pilot*, Marryat; Melville, Conrad; popular Forester / O’Brian naval careers; Defoe-era piracy precursors
- Overlaps war fiction, Robinsonade, travel, psychological novel — identity = immersive sea culture, not shore-only maritime economy

**Themes the page must carry**

- Social hierarchies aboard (officer vs for’ard; prize law; discipline)
- Psychological struggle in a hostile sea environment
- Masculinity / heroism models vary by era of writing — don’t paste one model onto all centuries
- Period research heavy: sail handling, disease, prize law, naval discipline

**Working checklist**

- Prize / mission that organizes the voyage
- Loyalty test under captain or code
- Boarding / storm / becalming set-piece
- Port temptation — land laws vs ship freedom
- Crew as people with debts, not disposable NPCs

**Obligatory pressures / moments**
- Ship as society
- Sea as engine
- Prize / mission
- Loyalty / mutiny geometry
- Boarding / storm / becalming
- Port temptation

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Theme-park pirates | Economy of violence + scarcity |
| Engines in 1715 | Wind/current as plot math |
| Crew NPCs | Specialize labor; name debts |
| Accuracy flex stalls character | Research under action |

## Hard

**Naval career vs outlaw pirate dial**

- Career progression inside navy/merchant discipline → naval adventure (Hornblower / Aubrey lineage)
- Predation, prize, temporary codes outside state law → pirate lane — still owes ship society realism
- Wartime sea → may hand off to when unit/conscience under orders bills first

**Shore vs sea perspective**

- Shore-centric novels that only feel maritime economy are not this shelf — characters must be immersed in nautical culture
- Women-aboard and below-deck working-class traditions exist; default all-officer gentlemen cast is a market habit, not a law

**When to break “made or broken” voyage shape**
- Serial career novels may postpone “broken” across books — each voyage still needs a test that costs
- Literary sea (Melville/Conrad) may prioritize spiritual/psychological ordeal — keep sea hostility load-bearing
