# Slipstream

Engine: cognitive dissonance / “fiction of strangeness” — genre furniture and literary technique bleed; the familiar made strange (or reverse) without full SF/F market contract.

## Light

Job: strain a realist frame with persistent weird pressure — estrangement without obligatory world bible.

Contract: effect more than shelf; may refuse novum explanation or genre ending promises; comps matter more than the word “slipstream” in queries.

Must-not-break:
- Genre bleed signal early — uncanny without full SF/F badge
- Keep one foot in recognizable realism so the skew registers
- Tone lock — neither pure mimetic nor secondary-world tour
- Avoid bait-and-switch into cozy hard-SF lecture
- Ending keeps the bleed (unless intentional clean tax)
- Don’t flatten Indigenous slipstream usages into generic weird lit

## Normal

**Genre shape**

- Term popularized via Bruce Sterling (*SF Eye*, 1989): writing that makes you feel very strange — 20th/21st-c sensibility (coinage credit also to Richard Dorsett in Sterling interview accounts)
- Kelly/Kessel (*Feeling Very Strange*): less a shelf than a literary effect (like horror or comedy) centered on cognitive dissonance
- SFE: “slipstream sf” for works that use SF devices but are not Genre SF
- Overlaps: metafiction, surrealism, magical realism, experimental — critics disagree what’s in the bag
- Distinct from pure category SF/F: may refuse novum explanation or genre ending promises

**Working checklist**

- Estrangement without obligatory world bible
- One persistent weird pressure straining realist frame
- Refusal of full system explanation (or explanation that doesn’t comfort)
- Identity / reality doubt scene
- Tone consistency beats random weirdness salad
- Comps > label in queries

**Obligatory pressures**
- Genre bleed signal early
- Realist frame strained by persistent weird pressure
- Refusal of full system explanation
- Identity / reality doubt scene
- Tone lock
- Ending that keeps the bleed

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Weirdness salad | One pressure, consistent tone |
| Dodge SF/F research | Earn the skew or rebill category SF/F |
| Appropriate Indigenous framing | Know Dillon/Vizenor sovereignty meanings — or don’t claim the term that way |
| Magical realism brick-face confusion | Route / |

## Hard

**Three uses of “slipstream” (do not conflate)**

| Use | Claim | Craft implication |
|-----|-------|-------------------|
| Sterling strangeness | Feel very strange; 20th-c sensibility | Affect/effect first; shelf optional |
| Kelly/Kessel effect | Cognitive dissonance as literary effect | Not a marketing category — design the dissonance |
| Indigenous slipstream (Dillon et al.) | Temporal sovereignty in past/present/future; Vizenor lineage | Specific sovereignty meanings — don’t flatten into generic weird |

- Priest on Kavan *Ice*: state of mind / approach outside categorisation — “otherness” like a distorting mirror
- Prototypes often cited: Kafka, Borges — estrangement without franchise worldbuilding

**Adjacent routing**

- Magical realism: supernatural treated as mundane in a realist social world — different contract than intrusion fantasy
- Experimental/postmodern: form games may produce slipstream effect without genre furniture
- Category SF/F with full explanation and world tour → not slipstream; don’t use the word to dodge research

**When to break “keep the bleed”**
- Late clean explanation OK only if the book taught that reveal as part of its dissonance game — otherwise rebill
