# Domestic thriller / intimate psychological suspense

Engine: danger already lives in the house. Threat is intimate — spouse, neighbor, family secret — not a ticking bomb downtown.

## Light

Job: anchor thriller pressure in domestic life — home, marriage, family, neighborhood.

Contract: intimate betrayal and household wrongness; distinct from broad thriller and from psych thriller when the cage is the household itself.

Must-not-break:
- House as character (decor, locks, rooms that reveal the marriage)
- Perfect-family cracks — public performance vs private reality
- Trapped ≠ passive — paralysis psychologically earned
- Twist grows from domestic soil
- “Spouse did it” is a starting point, not the whole architecture

## Normal

**Domestic vs psychological**

| | Domestic thriller | Psychological thriller |
|--|-------------------|------------------------|
| Anchor | Home, marriage, family, neighborhood | Protagonist’s mind / perception anywhere |
| Threat flavor | Intimate betrayal, household wrongness | Epistemic danger, identity, reality gap |
| Overlap | *Gone Girl* (both) | Can be non-domestic |

*Behind Closed Doors* (Paris) = specifically domestic; house as prison.

**Five craft truths**

1. House as character — setting reveals the marriage before anyone admits it
2. Perfect-family cracks — reader feels wrongness before they can name it
3. Trapped ≠ passive — grief, money, kids, immigration, gaslighting explain why “just leave” fails
4. Twist rooted in household anxieties (new baby, neighbors, trust)
5. Best books let the obvious suspect run, then reframe **why the household was already breaking**

**Tension mechanics**

- Subtext over jump scares
- Ordinary baseline makes deviation loud
- Weapon is often **information** (who has it, who’s denied it)
- Drain trust / identity / control / escape routes across acts
- Act Two = widen appearance vs reality without resolving it

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Passive cage | Earn the trap psychologically |
| Gore/action abandons intimacy | Keep household stakes load-bearing |
| Off-book conspiracy twist | Seed in domestic details |
| Unlikable without Want | Readable want/need under the mask |

## Hard

**Twist types that fit**

- Reframe whose story the reader was reading (victim/villain flip)
- Gradual secret drip vs single bomb — bomb must be plantable in domestic details
- Dual timelines / unreliable narrator: recontextualize earlier dinner-table scenes
- Climax may be psychological breakthrough or confrontation — paid for by household truth

**Parent-thriller tools**

- Hitchcock information privilege still applies 
- When epistemic threat outruns household cage → lean 
- Puzzle-heavy household secrets may borrow fair-play 

**When to break the house**
- Neighborhood / small-town expansion OK if intimate complicity remains the knife
- Crossing into horror: residual dread after household truth
