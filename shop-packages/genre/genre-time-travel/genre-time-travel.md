# Time travel

Engine: **cost and logic of moving in time** — paradox, predestination, alternate timelines, or tourist/war uses of the past/future; the mechanism’s rules are the novum that generates plot.

## Light

Job: make the time rule create unique obstacles — not a dashboard demo.

Contract: consequences of past/future travel load-bearing; one temporal metaphysics enforced; cost is emotional as well as causal.

Must-not-break:
- Rule card early: can you change the past? branching or single wire?
- Choose **one** metaphysics and enforce it
- Paradox / butterfly / predestination must have emotional stake
- Track who remembers what after branches/loops
- Return mismatch or traveler change — travel should alter someone
- Rules that flex for climax convenience break the book

## Normal

**Genre shape**
- Sometimes called chrono fiction / temporal fiction / chrono opera
- Modern mechanical popularization: Wells *The Time Machine* (1895)
- Focus usually on **consequences** — not the dashboard alone
- Social commentary via distancing: visit another era to estrange the present
- Fantasy cousins: time slip (uncontrolled), supernatural conveyance, ghost-as-past-contact

**Mechanism types (pick and lock)**

| Type | Trait | Craft note |
|------|-------|------------|
| **Time machine** | Controllable device | Patent the limits (range, fuel, passenger count) |
| **Time slip** | Unexplained marooning | Protagonist lacks control; mystery of return |
| **Time portal / gate** | Fixed link between points | Heinlein “By His Bootstraps” paradox playground |
| **Message from future** | Info only travels | Newspaper/SMS changes behavior → timeline stress |
| **Precognition / reverse living** | Experience without vehicle | Cassandra; causal loops; *Arrival* overlay |
| **Time loop** | Period resets; memories may stick | Learning montage; distinct from fixed causal loop |

**Themes / engines**
- Paradox — grandfather etc.; changing past vs fixed past
- Predestination / Cassandra — you can’t change it; you fulfill it
- Alternate timelines / dimensions — sidewise histories after change
- Butterfly effect — small past act, huge present rewrite
- Time tourism — ethics of spectators; time war — factions fighting history
- Identity — meeting selves; bootstrap objects/knowledge with no origin

**Obligatory pressures / moments**
- First jump cost: physical, psychological, social (language, disease, anachronism)
- Temptation to fix — personal loss vs timeline integrity
- Paradox pinch — plan that would erase the planner
- Butterfly bill — unintended consequence of a “small” change
- Moral audit — tourism, colonialism of the past, weaponized chronology

**Working checklist**
- Diagram timelines for yourself even if the book never shows the diagram
- Research target era if visiting history — AH rigor applies (, )
- Romance/thriller hybrids still need the time rule to create unique obstacles
- Distinguish time loop (resetting) from causal loop (unchanging bootstrap)

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Rules flex at climax | Enforce or foreshadow exception cost |
| Paradox as decoration | Emotional stake for erasure/change |
| Chronophysics dump | Character want under the rule |
| “Kill Hitler” tourism, no ethics/causality | Follow-through bill |
| Loop types confused | Pick reset vs bootstrap |

## Hard

**Metaphysics spectra (don’t mix midstream)**
- Fixed past / predestination vs free-change / branching — mixing without a stated bridge loses readers
- Message-only travel still needs timeline stress when information changes behavior
- Fantasy time-slip vs SF machine: control and explainability change mystery vs puzzle contracts

**Cross-lane retags**
- Changed past that becomes the “real” history of the novel → also 
- Uncontrolled threshold to another when/where → portal adjacency 
- Clock pressure without temporal mechanics → may just be 

**When to break “one wire” purity**
- Multiverse reveal OK if seeded; silent rewrite of earlier rules is not
- Predestination endings that reframe earlier “choices” must still feel earned, not author cheat
