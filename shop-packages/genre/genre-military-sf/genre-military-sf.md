# Military science fiction

Engine: **doctrine, unit, and the tech of war in a speculative setting** — protagonists usually inside a military (or mercenary) organization; conflict described with attention to tactics, weapons, logistics, and the soldier’s lived difference from civilian life.

## Light

Job: make doctrine, unit, and war-tech the engine — not civilian adventure with spaceship wallpaper.

Contract: POV near or in service culture; tactics/logistics/chain of command load-bearing; courage and cost complicated, not sermonized.

Must-not-break:
- Military personnel (career, draftee, or mercenary) as leads — or war is not the engine
- Detailed conflict geometry: belligerents, tactics, weapons, command
- Tech/doctrine shift can turn battles — limits and counters required
- Human cost ledger: wounds, moral injury, pyrrhic “wins”
- Not “bug shoots” / power fantasy without consequence
- Distinguish from space-opera spectacle when war lifestyle isn’t central

## Normal

**Genre shape**
- Hybrid of military fiction + SF tech (ships, energy weapons, powered armor, doctrine under new physics)
- Often remaps Earth wars onto planets/galaxies — battleships → space navies; infantry → marines
- Post-pulp: “more disciplined and realistic” armies than extravagant space-opera war pageants (SFE)
- Values cluster: duty, honor, sacrifice, loyalty, camaraderie — complicated, not sermonized

**Military SF vs space opera**

| | Military SF | Space opera |
|--|-------------|-------------|
| Focus | Warfare, doctrine, technical/military lifestyle | Adventure, melodrama, empire spectacle |
| Leads | Military personnel (career or draftee) | Often civilians / paramilitary |
| Failure mode | “Bug shoots” without human cost | Horse opera; empty armadas |

Hybrids common (*Starship Troopers*, Honorverse, *Battlestar*). Weber-style framing: beings “caught up in warfare and carnage,” not an excuse for simplistic solutions.

**Obligatory pressures / moments**
- Doctrine vs reality: plan meets fog, terrain (or vacuum), enemy adaptation
- Unit bond tested: squad/ship loyalty under loss or illegal order
- Logistics beat: ammo, fuel, medical, training — war as supply
- Weapon/tech debut that changes moral and tactical math
- Home-front dissonance: civilian misunderstanding; veteran return; policy vs mud
- Command choice: ends-justify-means temptation with named subordinates

**Working checklist**
- Enough real military texture to invent convincingly (ranks, after-action, NCO reality)
- Fight geography legible — 
- Speculative weapons need limits and counters
- Political theme OK — thesis ≠ tract
- Declare career-soldier contract early vs civilian opera leads

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Bug shoots / power fantasy | Doctrine + consequence |
| Infodump TOE tables | Human stakes under the tables |
| Military as monolith | Service rivalry, grunt vs brass |
| War porn, no aftermath | Cost ledger on-page |
| Ships fighting, cast not military | Retag opera/adventure |

## Hard

**Historical / thematic spectra**
- Precursors and poles: Heinlein *Starship Troopers*, Dickson Dorsai ethics-of-militarism; Vietnam-era counters (Haldeman *Forever War*, Spinrad, Harrison parody)
- SFE: Korea/UN intervention context → Vietnam polarization of the field (incl. 1968 *Galaxy* ads for/against the war) still shapes pro- and anti-glamour strains
- Mercenary fashion (Drake *Hammer’s Slammers*) vs space-navy / Hornblower-in-space (Weber Honorverse, Feintuch)
- Women writers’ substantial contribution (Moon, Traviss, Bujold military episodes) — don’t treat the lane as monoculture

**Subdivision: ex-military civilian ops**
- Battle experience applied to crime/law/tough ops (Morgan Kovacs; Bear Jenny Casey) — still draws MSF DNA; primary shelf may be crime/thriller

**When to break pure MSF labeling**
- If adventure/melodrama leads and service culture is costume → 
- Time cost of travel (relativistic/cryo) as war trauma is MSF-strengthening, not optional garnish (*Forever War* lineage)
