# Crime fiction (literary / commercial)

Engine: transgression and its aftermath — caper, heist, underworld, or moral fallout; mystery puzzle optional. Focus is what crime *does* to people and systems, not only who-dunnit.

## Light

Job: make the crime alter power, loyalty, or self-story — not decorative illegal flavor.

Contract: commercial crime often promises momentum + milieu; literary crime leans voice, theme, social x-ray. Solution may be known early; interest is execution, betrayal, consequence.

Must-not-break:
- Crime changes something structural (power / loyalty / self)
- Plan → complication → improvisation when heist/caper
- Betrayal geometry (who knows what)
- Aftermath cost runs after the job
- Don’t bait fair-play mystery if you never intend fair clues

## Normal

**Genre shape**

- Broad umbrella: mystery, detective, caper, gangster, heist, courtroom-adjacent, literary crime
- Distinct from fair-play mystery : who-dunnit optional
- Hardboiled/noir sibling → ; police system → 

**Working checklist**

- Ensemble underworld: roles, codes, debts — or lone operator pressure
- Heist/caper: competence porn with human leak points
- Aftermath novel valid: job over early; cost runs the book
- Moral architecture: who pays, who walks, what “winning” means

**Obligatory pressures**
- Job / score / underworld Want with a plan
- Crew or lone-operator pressure
- Betrayal geometry
- Heist/caper/operation set-piece
- Aftermath complication (money / heat / guilt)
- Justice or getaway ending that fits moral tone (noir vs caper)

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Cool aesthetic, no pressure | Name what the crime costs someone |
| Mystery bait-and-switch | Signal crime-aftermath contract early |
| Victims as props | Humanize who pays |
| Procedure cosplay without character | Keep Want/Need under the ops |

## Hard

**Mystery vs crime dial**

- If reader pleasure is fair clues → lean 
- If knowledge closes options / moral ledger dominates → lean 
- If system work (unit, protocol, courtbound evidence) dominates → 

**Literary vs commercial pressure**

- Commercial: momentum + milieu promises must be paid in set-pieces and heat
- Literary: voice/theme/social x-ray can slow the chase — don’t promise thriller clock then withhold
- Crossing into thriller intensity: escalate clock/danger without abandoning crime’s moral aftermath

**When to break the getaway ending**
- Noir contract allows partial/messy/downbeat “wins”
- Caper comedy may restore order — seed that lighter moral tone early
