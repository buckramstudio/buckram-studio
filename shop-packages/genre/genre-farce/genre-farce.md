# Farce (novel / comic mode)

Engine: escalating misunderstanding as a timing machine; implausible but rule-bound situations; physical and social doors slamming; speed toward collapse/reveal.

## Light

Job: accelerate lies and near-misses until the pile-up lands — timing is the engine, not a garnish.

Contract: comedy of manic pace; stock/exaggerated types OK if they turn; dramatic irony (reader knows); slight realism inside absurdity so consequences threaten; fast denouement.

Must-not-break:
- Establish the lie / secret early; each chapter adds a plate to spin
- Pace must accelerate — awkward without speed is not farce
- Characters stay earnest about absurd stakes
- Don’t explain every joke
- Denouement unties knots fast; don’t moralize the pile-up away
- Distinct from satire’s argument target and from gentle comic novel’s warmer pace

## Normal

**Genre shape**

- Theatre-born: stock types, mistaken identity, concealment, manic pace, bedroom/backstage subtypes
- Novel farce translates slamming doors into chapter timing, POV near-misses, and escalating lies
- Uses bawdiness, absurdity, parody, physical comedy; plot delivered with increasing speed toward denouement
- Despite absurd situations, maintains slight degree of realism and narrative continuity — distinguishes farce from pure absurdist/fantastical collapse
- Often episodic or location-bound in stage tradition; novels may widen space but keep pressure geography tight

**Craft checklist**

- Lie / secret planted early
- Dramatic irony: reader knows, characters miss each other by inches
- Stereotypes OK if they *turn*; pure cartoon without want tires
- Keep slight realism inside absurdity — consequences must threaten
- Public exposure threat forces worse lies
- Collapse pile-up timed to land; fast denouement

**Obligatory pressures**
- Lie / secret planted early
- Plate-spin acceleration — each act adds a complication
- Near-miss dramatic irony
- Public exposure threat
- Collapse pile-up timed to land
- Fast denouement that unties without sermon

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Mean-spirited pile-on | Add affection or satirical aim  |
| Pace never accelerates | Cut explanation; add timed near-misses |
| Explaining every joke | Trust dramatic irony |
| Characters wink at absurdity | Keep them earnest; let reader hold the irony |

## Hard

**Stage subtypes → novel translation**

| Stage type | Novel equivalent |
|------------|------------------|
| Bedroom farce | Adjoining rooms, phones, group chats — proximity engines |
| Backstage farce | Production/office/wedding logistics as slamming-door machine |
| Tragic farce | Catastrophe still timed as comedy; tone edge toward dark comedy |
| Disguise / role-swap | Identity concealment for romance or money — social doors |

- Masks, gender/social role swaps, wordplay and slapstick need **adept comic timing** on the page (beat placement, chapter cuts)
- Etymology/history (French “stuffing”; medieval interludes) explains the stuffed-in improvisation energy — useful when pace feels padded rather than stuffed with complications

**Farce vs neighbors**

- Satire needs a named power gap; farce needs acceleration — you can combine, but don’t bill farce if the joke never speeds
- Absurdist/fantastical genres drop the slight-realism floor; farce keeps continuity so stakes threaten
- Gentle comic novel: warmer pace, lower plate count

**When to break manic speed**
- Brief breath scenes OK if they load the next plate; sustained lull breaks the farce contract — retitle as comic novel
