# Fantasy

Engine: the world and its rules (especially magic) — wonder paid for with consistency.

## Light

Job: make the fantastic *system or world* create conflict, not decorate it.

Contract: reader expects an invented or magically altered reality whose rules (even soft ones) constrain what cleverness can solve; subtypes change scale and intrusion style.

Must-not-break:
- Magic/world rules teach before climax use (First Law pressure)
- Limitations / costs more interesting than power lists (Second Law)
- Expand ramifications before adding new systems (Third Law)
- Naming / linguistic consistency across cultures
- Each POV needs distinct voice when multi-POV
- Pick a subtype contract early (epic, urban, portal, S&S…) — don’t blur shelves by accident

## Normal

**Core model — subtype engines**
- **High / epic:** secondary world, often quest-shaped, map-page expectation — 
- **Low:** fantastic intrusion into familiar/primary world (or scarce-magic grit — name which sense) — 
- **Urban:** magic against contemporary/city grain; secrecy/masquerade frequent — 
- **Portal / isekai:** threshold into other-world rules — 
- **Sword & sorcery:** personal-scale pulp adventure — 
- **Dark fantasy:** horror-tinged fantastic (≠ automatic grimdark) — 
- **Grimdark / mythic / cozy / LitRPG:** see dedicated notes; hub 

**Sanderson’s Laws (working spine)**
1. Ability to solve conflict with magic ∝ how well the reader understands that magic
2. Limitations > Powers (can’t / won’t / costs — prefer costs that force clever scenes)
3. Expand what you have before you add something new (depth > kitchen-sink width)

Soft magic (wonder; magic creates problems) vs hard magic (explicit rules; cleverness with known tools) vs middle (hard within a book, soft across a series).

**Working checklist**
- Don’t give heroes a new power for the climax; use what was shown

## Hard

**Soft ↔ hard continuum (not binary)**
- Soft (Tolkien / much Martin): mysticism; magic rarely solves cleanly (else deus ex)
- Hard (rules explicit — Asimov robots as analogy; many superhero sets): character cleverness with known tools
- Middle (e.g. Rowling per Sanderson essay): hard within a book, soft across the series
- Campbell-era SF-vs-fantasy jab is historical noise for craft — use the continuum for fair play, not tribal labels

**Second Law toolkit beyond magic**
- Limitations / weaknesses / costs apply to character can’ts, harsh worlds, hard plots
- Prefer costs that force theme and relationship pressure, not only mana meters

**When to break “expand before add”**
- Series reset / new culture book may introduce a system — still interconnect thematically
- Portal / isekai: far-side rules can be new *to the character* if consistent and taught
- If horror-tinged, dread logic may trump taxonomy — see /
