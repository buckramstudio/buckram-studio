# Dark erotica (vs dark romance)

Engine: sexual/moral darkness as the experiential core — discomfort and arousal coexist; romantic HEA is **not** required. Distinct from dark romance, which still owes central love + optimistic couple landing.

## Light

Job: commit to advertised darkness as psychology + arousal, not shock checklist — without baiting romance HEA buyers.

Contract: sexual/moral intensity is the spine; couple permanence optional; content notes honest.

Must-not-break:
- Darkness ≠ heat (can be dark closed-door or explicit and emotionally bright)
- Commit to the darkness you advertise — pull-back after dark setup breaks trust
- Psychology over shock pile
- Consent honesty with the reader (notes); don’t market as fluffy erotic romance
- Harm meant as harm must read as harm with consequence
- Illegal content (including anything involving minors) out of scope — no craft guidance for that

## Normal

**Boundary table**

| Form | Central focus | Romantic ending |
|------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Dark romance** | Love story shaped by danger/obsession/power imbalance | Generally **required** (HEA/HFN) |
| **Dark erotica** | Sexual transgression, intensity, moral complexity | **Not** required |
| **BDSM/kink erotica** | Negotiated power exchange (SSC/RACK craft) | Optional; consent architecture differs from “dark” coercion aesthetics |
| **Horror + sex** | Dread/violation as horror engine | Usually not romance |

- Retail blur: many “dark” books are dark *romance* misfiled — define by ending + spine
- “Dark” ≠ abuse without consequence

**Contested labels (high level — no how-to)**

- Noncon/dubcon as market tags are contested; platforms/processors often restrict them
- Ethical/legal/platform risk is real: bait-and-switch, glorification without framing, illegal content are out of scope
- **CNC** is a *negotiated kink frame* among consenting adults — craft with diegetic contract , not unexamined coercion
- This file does **not** provide guidance for writing non-consent as a genre goal

**Reader promises**

1. Commit to advertised darkness
2. Psychology over shock checklist
3. Consent honesty with the reader
4. Arousal + unease — only unease → horror lean; only arousal → standard erotica

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Dark romance without HEA sold as romance | Recategorize as dark erotica / dark fiction |
| Abuse-as-kink with no negotiation frame | Negotiated CNC craft or name harm as harm |
| Shock pile without character | Motive, consequence, interiority |
| Content-note cowardice | Specific warnings; heat ≠ darkness disclosure |
| BDSM readers baited into non-negotiated cruelty | Honest tags; see kink file |

## Hard

**Aftercare as thematic choice**

- Aftercare may be denied, twisted, or incomplete in dark erotica — that’s a **choice with thematic cost**, not “skipping craft”
- Kink with safewords/negotiation ≠ dark-erotica coercion aesthetics — don’t conflate for cover copy
- If the story depicts harm, narrative must not gaslight the reader about whether harm occurred

**Edge triage**

- Taboo-as-social-forbidden without coercion aesthetics → 
- Literary transgression / erotic literature broader than commercial dark erotica → 
- When to break “HEA optional”: if you still want romance-shelf distribution, you likely need dark *romance* craft instead
