# YA / coming-of-age craft

Category defined by protagonist age + narrative stance for teen emotional experience — not “any book with a teenager.”

## Light

Job: write *for* teen emotional experience — present-tense feeling, total-feeling stakes, teen agency; adults rarely solve the core problem.

Contract: protagonists typically ~14–18; inexperience codes tone; heat matches YA shelf (full explicit usually pushes adult).

Must-not-break:
- Stance: for teen emotional experience, not only adolescence as adult subject
- Stakes feel total from inside (breakup, humiliation, rejection) — honor teen scale
- Adults present but meaningful choices belong to the teen
- Voice ≠ slang pack; ≠ thirty-year-old philosopher in a locker
- Interiority immediate, specific, low reflective distance
- Coming-of-age core: identity, authority conflict, self-determination, first loves/failures

## Normal

**Category boundaries**

- Readers often broader (adults buy YA heavily — market fact, not craft excuse for adult reflection)
- YA vs MG: see 
- New Adult never fully stabilized — 
- Content: violence, substances, non-explicit sexuality OK; check comps + spice contract
- YA dystopia still needs this file’s voice/agency 

**Voice: inexperience as engine**

- Approaching adult-scale events without long practice — bravado of never-failed + anxiety of never-succeeded
- Teens often live in **present-tense feeling** — cafeteria look *is* the catastrophe
- Diagnostic: cut sentences that only establish adult-style context/history; remaining page must still carry pressure
- First person common; tight limited third works — voice load-bearing either way

**Emotional logic, agency, dialogue**

- Emotional amplification is a feature; external stakes still need personal heartbeat
- Mentors may guide; deus ex parent/teacher swoop = category friction unless intentional with cost
- Authenticity = mindset in transition more than jargon; contact with real teens beats memory alone
- Length ~60–90k; momentum; usually one–two subplot threads

**Coming-of-age beyond marketing YA**

- Bildungsroman / initiation: innocence → experience; social role negotiated
- Can live on adult literary shelves — reflective distance / retrospective frame sorts the shelf
- Threshold moments: first moral compromise, first adult-sized responsibility, irreversible knowledge
- Lie/Truth arcs map cleanly 

| Symptom | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Adult in teen costume | Present-tense stakes; cut reflective distance |
| Slang caricature | Emotional logic + specific observation |
| Adults solve Act 3 | Return agency to protagonist |
| Soft stakes | Amplify personal cost; honor teen scale |
| Trend checklist plot | Authenticity over trope bingo |
| Wrong shelf heat | Match intimacy to YA contract |
| Adult nostalgia novel | Check narrative stance / intended reader |

## Hard

**Stance triage (YA vs adult-with-teen-POV)**

- *Catcher*-style adult shelf often uses adolescence as subject with reflective frame — craft signals sort the market
- Protect teen readership when that’s the target: journeys authentic *to teens*, not only crossover adults

**When to break “adults at distance”**
- Loving-but-ineffective / complicit / antagonistic adults are tools — solving Act 3 for the teen breaks category unless the cost is the theme (failed autonomy)
- Heat: pushing full explicit while claiming YA — retag adult or honor closed-door/YA sensual contract
