# Romantasy / fantasy romance

Engine: fantasy machinery + romance machinery both load-bearing — magic, courts, quests pressure trust/power/belonging; yearning pressures the plot. Labels disagree; position by primary promise.

## Light

Job: keep magic and courtship co-escalating so neither is wallpaper.

Contract: declare romance-primary (HEA/HFN) vs fantasy-primary (quest/court may defer couple landing) — then keep that promise.

Must-not-break:
- Practical test: if romance resolved mid-book, is a complete story left?
- Magic costs/limits must change couple choices
- Trope delivery through secondary-world rules, not CR pasted onto castles
- Heat lane disclosed (BookTok romantasy often assumes steamy; sweet must be labeled)
- Don’t break magic to force the kiss
- Comps lead with trope+HEA *or* world+stakes — not muddle

## Normal

**Label chaos (use a practical test)**

| Promise | Primary question | HEA/HFN | Typical craft bias |
|---------|------------------|---------|-------------------|
| **Fantasy romance** (romance shelf) | Do these people land together? | Required | Romance beats precise; fantasy serves couple obstacles |
| **Romantic fantasy / “romantasy” as fantasy-first** | Does the quest/war/court plot resolve? | Optional / series-deferred | World + plot must stand if romance thinner |
| **True co-engine hybrid** | Both arcs escalate each other | Often expected by crossover readers | Neither is decoration |

Wiki note: “fantasy romance” / “romantic fantasy” publishing lines historically split by which emphasis dominates.

**Reader promises (romance-primary lane)**

- Central love drives emotional climax; optimistic couple ending for category romance
- Magic costs pressure trust, power imbalance, belonging, political marriage, curse/bond consent
- World worth inhabiting — not cardboard castles behind clinch poses

**Craft braid**

- Scenes that advance **both** magic/plot stakes and relationship risk
- Sanderson-style consistency still applies 
- Series: romance-primary often new couple per book; fantasy-primary may defer final HEA — still need meaningful emotional movement per installment
- POV: single/dual common on romance shelf; wider cast on fantasy-first epics
- Length: fantasy-first often longer; don’t pad world tours that stall yearning

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Fantasy wallpaper on CR beats | Magic must change couple choices |
| Quest novel sold as HEA romance | Label fantasy-first; or earn couple landing |
| Mating bond / prophecy skips growth | Destiny pressures; choice still arcs |
| Info-dump magic system | Teach rules when they hurt/help the relationship |
| Heat bait-and-switch | Cover/blurb/comps match interior |
| Wrong shelf comps | Lead with one primary promise |

## Hard

**Co-engine diagnostics**

- Dual Black Moments: political/quest loss *and* relational rupture that reframe each other
- Forced proximity / enemies-to-lovers must be enforced by world rules (treaty, war camp, curse), not coincidence tourism
- When to break HEA mid-series: only under fantasy-primary positioning with installment emotional payoff — do not bait romance-shelf buyers with open couple ending

**Bond / prophecy edge**

- Fated mates in secondary world inherit PNR craft: attraction ≠ trust; political cost of the bond is plot
- Prophecy that removes agency kills romantasy yearning — prophecy should *constrain* choices, not write the kiss
