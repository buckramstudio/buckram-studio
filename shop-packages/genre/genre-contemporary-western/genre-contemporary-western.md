# Contemporary western / neo-Western

Engine: frontier psychology in a modern West — land, ranch, border, small-city crime, extractive economy; law still thin or compromised; landscape dwarfs human plans.

## Light

Job: keep Old West isolation / code / landscape pressure load-bearing after the six-guns leave — modern systems update the board, not the psychology.

Contract: neo-Western / contemporary Western uses Western themes, archetypes, and motifs in present (or near-present) settings; not “cowboys with cell phones” cosplay.

Must-not-break:
- Landscape or thin-authority geography still shapes major choices
- Code tested against modern systems (DEA, corporate ag, tourism, oil/gas, tribal sovereignty — research locally)
- Antagonist motives tied to land / money / survival, not only black-hat chic
- Violence costs in a world with forensics and phones
- Quiet character beats between set-pieces
- Avoid period Western dialogue pasted onto suburbs

## Normal

**Genre shape**

- Contemporary / neo / post / urban Western: Old West mentality progressing into late 20th–21st c., or Western motifs relocated (e.g. urban Western, Appalachian neo-Western)
- Touchstones often cited: *No Country for Old Men*, *Hell or High Water*; TV lineages (*Justified*, *Breaking Bad* as contemporary Western framing)
- Hybrids: crime Western, literary ranch novel, border thriller, eco-Western; space Western when SF is secondary to Western plot pressure
- Displacement theme: Old West–type characters struggling in a “civilized” world that rejects their justice brand

**Neo-Western identifying themes** (film/TV criticism synthesis)

1. Lack of rules — morals guided by character/audience instinct more than governance
2. Search for justice
3. Remorse / consequences of action (binds to broader Western)

**Working checklist**

- Landscape pressure on every major choice (distance, drought, fire, empty miles to help)
- Law vs custom in a thin-policed space
- Outsider or returnee confronting local power
- Violence threat that feels modern (meth, cartels, land grab) not only six-guns
- Justice ending negotiated with neo-Western ambiguity

**Obligatory pressures / moments**
- Land / water / ranch / reservation / border as moral pressure *now*
- Law vs custom beat
- Landscape set-piece that decides a choice
- Competence / virility under public pressure (genre convention — use or critique deliberately)
- Remorse / cost after violence

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Cosplay modernity | Keep frontier psychology; change infrastructure only |
| Indigenous nations as scenery | Agents with sovereignty and motives |
| Any-city crime plot | Make landscape / thin law load-bearing |
| Nostalgia without present stakes | Extractive economy / border / water *now* |

## Hard

**Geography dial**

- Need not stay in the traditional American West — urban Westerns and non-West settings count when isolation + weak authority + justice search remain
- Space / SF Western adjacency: SF secondary; Western characteristics drive plotlines — else retag SF

**Traditional vs neo routing**

- Period hats + Old West tech → 
- Same psychology, phones/forensics/cartels → stay here
- Crime/noir moral ledger dominates landscape → check 

**When to break classic showdown**
- Ambiguous justice endings are on-contract if remorse and consequence remain
- Patriarchal competence-through-violence convention is a critical tell — either earn it or invert it with eyes open
