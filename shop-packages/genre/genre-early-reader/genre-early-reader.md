# Early reader

Engine: controlled vocabulary + confidence loops — short chapters or spreads that let new readers succeed.

## Light

Job: bridge picture-book dependence and independent chapter-book stamina without breaking decoding confidence.

Contract: clear kid-scaled problem; repetition builds mastery; illustration often supports hard words/jokes; resolution the child can feel they “got.”

Must-not-break:
- Readable hook on page one (want or funny problem)
- One primary plotline; subplot minimal
- Try → setback → try again confidence loop
- Vocabulary serves success, not flex
- No adult-only twist as the only engine
- Age-band content check 

## Normal

**Format ladder (don’t collapse)**

| Format | Rough ages | Word-count guide | Art role |
|--------|------------|------------------|----------|
| Early / leveled readers | ~4–8 | often &lt;1,500; controlled vocab | heavy; supports decoding |
| Early chapter books | ~6–9 | ~4,000–15,000 | frequent B&W; visual rest |
| Upper chapter / young MG | ~7–10 | ~15,000–30,000 | occasional |
| Middle grade | ~8–12 | ~20,000–55k+ | optional |

- Early readers: specialized phonics/leveling craft; editors treat differently from chapter books
- Series friendly: recurring cast, predictable structure, rising challenge
- Humor, friendship, school, pets, gentle mystery common; avoid dense worldbuilding

**Working checklist**
- Dialogue and action over long description
- Name repetition and clear pronoun antecedents
- Humor physical or situational more than ironic narration
- Chapter/section ends that invite “one more”
- Illustration support for hard words / jokes
- Problem a child can hold — lost item, friend fight, tryout nerves

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Vocabulary flex that breaks confidence | Context + repeat; cut show-off diction |
| PB length with CB pretensions (or reverse) | Match format band |
| Adult satire as only engine | Kid-scaled want + visible success |
| Scary stakes without recovery | Restore agency/hope path |

## Hard

**Stamina & chapter psychology (from chapter-book craft)**
- Chapter ≈ length of many early readers the child just left — each unit needs a small satisfying success
- White space, larger type, short paragraphs = psychological progress (“I finished a chapter”)
- Gentle cliffhangers OK; many kids stop between chapters — don’t require cliff-only addiction
- Listening/speaking vocab > reading vocab — introduce tougher words in context, define nearby, repeat

**Sentence craft edges**
- Average sentence length often in the teens; occasional longer OK if flanked by short
- Polysyllabic OK if familiar (*mysterious*); balance wordy patches
- Avoid dense paragraphs, long clause stacks, unexplained jargon, heavy inner monologue
- Editor test (chapter-book side): natural and age-appropriate, not a Lexile spreadsheet — unless the book is explicitly leveled

**When hybrids fail**
- Picture-book counterpoint story in the art while prose carries a different plot → early-reader confusion (art should support decoding, not compete)
- Upper-MG themes in early-reader packaging → gatekeeper rejection and reader burnout
