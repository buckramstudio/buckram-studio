# Fairy-tale / mythic retelling

Engine: recognizable source machinery + deliberate innovation angle. Reader hears the old story under the new one; standalone completeness still required.

## Light

Job: keep the conversation with the original audible while the new book stands alone — write the angle in one sentence before drafting.

Contract: recognizable + standalone + legally clear (public domain / transformed) + familiar-with-twist.

Must-not-break:
- Know why the original worked before changing it
- If the ending differs, the foundation through the book must differ — no tacked-on twist
- Hunt variants beyond Disney beat sheets
- Preserve, carefully translate, or consciously replace cultural context — no sacred material as generic fairy dust
- Too faithful = reprint; too free = false advertising if marketed as retelling
- Easter-egg novel that doesn’t stand alone fails

## Normal

**Retelling vs reimagining vs fanfic**

| Approach | Keep | Innovate | Skill stress |
|----------|------|----------|--------------|
| Faithful retelling | Most structural/psych layers (≈5–7) | Voice, setting, or POV (≈1–3) | Restraint + voice |
| Reimagining | Few core layers (≈1–3) | Vision, structure, theme | Structural integrity |
| Loose retelling | Recognizable motifs/traits | Main plot may diverge | Standalone story |
| Close retelling | Same events/order, small variations | New lens | Precision of parallels |

- Folklore *is* retelling culture (ATU tale-types)
- Fanfic celebrates still-copyrighted characters; commercial retellings prefer public domain / transformed enough — research jurisdiction; not legal advice

**Contract with the reader**

1. Recognizable — major elements audible
2. Standalone — satisfying without source
3. Legal — public domain / cleared transformation
4. Familiar + twist

**Analyze before drafting**

- Major plot points; setting/context; character functions (helper, donor, villain, prize); theme/lesson; signature motifs/objects

**Angle catalog**

- New POV; antagonist as protagonist; race/class/gender/cultural lens; new era/geography; genre switch; combine two tale-types; raise stakes/agency

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Change climax without rebuilding causality | Rebuild foundation |
| Unrecognizable “retelling” on famous title | Restore anchors or drop the claim |
| Only Disney beat sheet | Hunt variants |
| Copying stereotypes without critique | Invert or replace deliberately |
| Easter-egg only | Standalone story first |

## Hard

**Cultural context non-negotiable**

- When borrowing outside your tradition, research variants and harm patterns; living cultures aren’t free loot tables
- Ending rule is structural: different ending demands different causality chain from early acts

**When to break recognizability**
- Loose retelling / motif-new mythic fiction may market without naming the source — then don’t promise “a retelling of X” in blurb
- Mythpunk transformation can shatter tale-type order — still need emotional clarity
