# Biographical historical fiction

Engine: a real figure’s life-arc (or a decisive span) structures the novel; invention fills gaps but must respect documented spine; the drama is constraint of the known + earned speculation.

## Light

Job: dramatize a documented life-spine without Wikipedia-with-dialogue — invention honest, systems visible, private camera where public record is dry.

Contract: biographical novel = fictional account of a contemporary or historical person’s life; details trimmed/reimagined for novel needs (sometimes labeled semi-biographical by historicity). Reader arrives with prior knowledge and will fact-check famous beats.

Must-not-break:
- Track undisputed facts / debated scholarship / invented connective tissue
- Author’s note honesty about invention
- Choose the slice: cradle-to-grave vs hinge years
- Interiority period-plausible without museum voice
- Systems the figure moved inside (not great-man tourism alone)
- Ending honors known fate or clearly marks alternate speculation

## Normal

**Genre shape**

- Named historical persons as protagonists; rose to prominence 1930s (Graves, Mann, Stone, Feuchtwanger) — popular then critically contested, later more accepted
- Often overlaps historical fiction or alternate history; some works only superficially biographical while another genre engine dominates (signal the hybrid)
- Frequently foundation for biopics — same distortion risk: over-reversed villains, entertainment over truth
- Ethics overlap real-people craft  even when subjects are long dead — reputational and descendant stakes remain

**Working checklist**

- Documented spine beats the reader may know — dramatize, don’t skip
- Secondary characters (servants, spouses, rivals) often carry emotional camera when public record is dry
- Disclose major invention in advance when distortion risk is high (wiki guidance: give necessary information to the reader)
- Avoid inventing atrocities/affairs that smear without disclosure
- Pick malleable gaps — plot that cannot bend because every date is sacred stalls drama

**Obligatory pressures / moments**
- Documented spine beat dramatized
- Invention disclosed in gaps
- Private camera when record is dry
- System pressure (court, war, gender law)
- Reputation / legacy stakes
- Ending vs known fate

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Timeline + dialogue frosting | Scene pressure; cut brochure |
| TED-talk psyche in period dress | Period content of thought |
| Undisclosed smear invention | Cut or disclose in note |
| Sacred-date paralysis | Choose hinge years / gaps |

## Hard

**Historicity spectrum**

- Semi-biographical label when resemblance is partial — use when another genre engine (horror, romance, AH) supersedes retelling
- Alternate-history biographical: mark the break early
- Critically, invention for artistic need is the genre’s tool — not a license for undetectable libel of the living or reckless descendant harm

**Camera & agency**

- Great-man tourism fails when systems disappear — show institutions shaping the figure
- Private/peripheral POVs can be more novelistic than the famous mind if records are thin — still honor the spine

**When to break known fate**
- Speculative endings / AH require clear contract
- Biopic-style “interesting” reversals of character that erase documented harm → disclose or don’t
