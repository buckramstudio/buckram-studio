# Mystery / crime

Engine: the puzzle. Reader plays along.

## Light

Job: let the reader race the detective — every decisive clue must be on the board before the reveal.

Contract: nature of the crime stays mysterious until the end; closed circle of suspects with credible motive + opportunity; solution by deduction from facts already shown (or howcatchem: culprit known, pleasure = catching/proving).

Must-not-break:
- Fair play: detective’s solution uses only information the reader could access
- Red herrings plausible and ultimately resolvable
- Reveal recontextualizes what was already shown — not a last-page dump
- Victim matters; motive is human
- Don’t bait puzzle pleasure if you never intend fair clues

## Normal

**Genre shape**
- Event (often murder) remains mysterious until the end; suspects each get credible motive + opportunity
- Detective fiction emphasis: puzzle/suspense + logical solution; contrast hardboiled (action/grit may dominate the puzzle)
- Whodunit: reader may beat the sleuth; howcatchem (inverted): culprit shown early
- Cozy / locked-room / PI / procedural / legal are sibling shelves — see table

**Working checklist**
- Plant true clues early; pair with misdirection
- Clue audit before publish: every fact the solution needs must appear before the reveal
- Sidekick/Watson: don’t hide their thoughts; avoid luck-as-genius for the detective
- When stakes leave the puzzle for continuous clock dread → check

## Hard

**Knox’s Decalogue (fair-play Golden Age)**

Mystery’s main interest = unravelling a mystery whose elements are clearly presented early; curiosity satisfied at the end.

1. Criminal mentioned early; not someone whose thoughts the reader has shared
2. No supernatural agencies (as solution)
3. At most one secret room/passage
4. No undiscovered poisons / appliances needing long end explanation
5. No exoticized stock-villain figure (period wording of the rule was racist — keep the craft intent: no stereotype shortcut)
6. No lucky accidents or unaccountable intuitions for the detective
7. Detective must not be the criminal
8. Detective must declare discovered clues
9. Watson-sidekick: don’t hide their thoughts; intelligence slightly below average reader
10. Twins/doubles only if prepared for

**Van Dine’s Twenty Rules (companion code)**

1928 *American Magazine* article — same spirit as Knox: reader as co-detective; no cheap withholding. Use as diagnostic pressure, not sacred law for every modern shelf.

**When to break fair-play purity**
- Hardboiled/noir: atmosphere, justice, moral damage may outrank puzzle completeness — retag toward if clues stop being the pleasure
- Howcatchem inverts concealment of identity — still owes fairness on *how* catch/prove works
- Thriller pace overlay OK if fair-play plants still hold when the engine is still “solve”
