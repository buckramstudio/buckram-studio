# New Adult (genre engine)

Protagonists roughly **18–29** navigating first independence — older-YA emotional immediacy with adult agency and (often) adult heat. Category never fully stabilized in Big Five shelving; craft still useful for voice, stakes, and comps.

## Light

Job: write first-threshold adulthood — independence pressure with adult consequences.

Contract: ~18–29 life stage; legal-adult stakes stick (debt, eviction, job, pregnancy, reputation); emotional volume high; heat often sensual→spicy but not mandatory.

Must-not-break:
- Adult agency — not high school with beer
- Independence / identity questions under the plot (college, first job, enlistment, gap year)
- Heat honesty: teen POV + explicit heat is not “NA”
- Optimistic couple ending only if romance-shelved (HEA/HFN rules apply)
- Don’t fake thirty-five-year-old insight in a sophomore skull
- Midlife fulfillment ≠ NA — that’s WF adjacency

## Normal

**What “New Adult” meant**
- Term popularized by St. Martin’s Press (2009) call for “older YA” / fiction marketable as adult with YA-like focus
- Target marketing age ~18–29; lucrative YA–adult crossover readership
- Themes: leaving home, sexuality, college/early career, identity without teen institutional scaffolding
- Early 2010s boom via self-pub → trad acquisition; later many titles simply shelve as **adult romance** or **romantasy** with college-aged leads
- Debate: real category vs marketing label — write the life-stage clearly; let comps decide the spine

**NA vs YA vs adult**

| | **YA** | **New Adult** | **Adult** (general / romance / WF) |
|--|--------|---------------|-------------------------------------|
| Protagonist age | ~14–18 | ~18–25 (sometimes to ~29) | Any; often 25+ emotional posture |
| Institutional frame | High school, guardians, curfews | College, first apartment, first job, enlistment, gap year | Established career/family or midlife reset |
| Agency | Adults at structural distance; teen solves core problem | Legal adult — consequences stick | Full adult systems |
| Heat default | Sweet→warm; explicit rare for teen shelf | Often sensual→spicy; sex as exploration theme | Any heat by subgenre |
| Ending romance | HFN common | HEA or HFN; “forever” can feel early — earn it | Per genre |

**Recurring independence tropes** (toolkit, not mandatory)
- Roommate / dorm / shared-house forced proximity; hometown vs campus identity split
- First serious sexual relationship; student debt / failing out / major vs passion
- First job / internship power imbalance — consent craft critical
- Found family renegotiating blood family; mental health first diagnosis without parental buffer
- Romance-heavy NA pairs these with category tropes — independence pressure should still *bite*

**Heat expectations**
- NA *can* be closed-door; commercially, contemporary NA romance often lands sensual–spicy
- Sex as theme (learning bodies, communication, bad/enthusiastic sex) fits life stage — still needs scene purpose
- Power-imbalance heat: on-page negotiation and consequence
- If sexual journey *is* the whole spine without HEA requirement → erotica lane 

**Working checklist**
- Keep YA’s emotional volume; add adult logistical stakes
- Peer chorus matters; professors/bosses complicate rather than “parent-solve”
- Identity experiment allowed to fail mid-book — practice adulthood, not mastery
- Pitch often as contemporary romance / romantasy / commercial fiction with age + comps

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Reads like high school with beer | Irreversible adult consequences |
| Reads like WF with a young face | First-threshold identity vs midlife fulfillment |
| Heat shocks YA fans | Age up cast or cool heat; don’t call it YA |
| Only sex, no independence plot | Deepen life-stage conflict or own erotica |
| “Forever” at 19 feels false | Strong HFN; earn HEA with concrete shared future |

## Hard

**Category instability (decision-changing)**
- Many agents never opened a formal NA box — structure and comps matter more than the label
- Speculative NA (romantasy with twenty-something leads) still needs independence/identity under the magic
- Adult novel with a 19-year-old POV ≠ NA if stance is retrospective literary study of youth
- YA with dual-enrollment 17-year-old still reads YA if school/guardian stakes dominate

**Cross-lane heat × age**
- After *Fifty Shades* press equated NA with “sexed-up YA” — craft truth: heat is dialable; age-band is not
- Erotic romance / erotica boundaries: HEA required vs optional (, )

**When to break “college only”**
- Enlistment, trade school, gap-year labor, caregiving for siblings can carry NA if first-independence is the engine
