# Upper middle grade (upper MG)

Engine: older MG voice with slightly sharper stakes — still kid agency and hope-forward, closer to teen complexity without full YA autonomy/heat contracts.

## Light

Job: serve ~10–13/14 readers with sharper social/moral pressure while keeping MG hope and kid solution space.

Contract: protagonists often 11–14; friends/family/immediate world still center; romance beyond crush usually wrong shelf; hope/agency ending typical.

Must-not-break:
- Kid solves the core problem — adults don’t swoop the climax
- Peer / identity pressure feels total for the protagonist’s world
- Hopeful turn earned (not magic eraser)
- Romance: crush/handhold territory — not YA dating plot
- Content milder than YA on sex/violence/drugs (imprint lines vary)
- No adult narrator condescension

## Normal

**Band facts**
- Middle grade: literature for ~8–12; distinct from early readers/chapter books and from YA (~12–18)
- Wiki content sketch (baseline, imprint-dependent): MG tends to avoid profanity, sexual activity, romantic behavior beyond kissing, and realistic/graphic violence (fantasy violence more tolerated)
- Focus stays friends, family, immediate surroundings even when external stakes are high
- Voice can handle irony, moral gray, grief, social cruelty — still usually ends with forward light

**Obligatory pressures**
- Friendship or family fracture with repair or redefined belonging
- Moral choice with visible cost
- Setback that isn’t cute — real embarrassment, loss, fear
- Adult world imperfect — helpers flawed; kid still acts
- Ending agency — protagonist caused the change

**Working checklist**
- Keep kid solution space
- Voice: smarter/funnier OK; cynicism-without-care breaks MG
- Length/complexity can rise vs core MG; info-dump still death
- Sensitivity pass for bullying, grief, identity
- Genre hybrids (fantasy, mystery, realistic) share MG hope/agency contract

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| YA themes with MG packaging | Cut heat/autonomy or re-shelf YA |
| Hopeless ending as default | Restore agency/hope thread |
| Adult condescension | Older-friend stance; concrete emotion |
| Sharper stakes that remove kid agency | Return climax choices to the kid |

## Hard

**MG↔YA border (Marie Lamba / category craft)**
- Word count and protagonist age alone don’t decide shelf — content and emotional centrality do
- Upper MG can touch grief, injustice, social cruelty with hope/agency scaffolding; YA can center autonomy, identity labyrinths, and romance heat
- Gatekeepers (parents, teachers, librarians) drive MG discovery more than teen peer networks — packaging and treatment affect access 

**Voice / POV edges**
- First person or close third preferred; multi-POV rare / limited in upper MG
- Present-focused, concrete > abstract; genuine emotion without adult over-analysis
- Humor + heart: laughs that erase cost undercut stakes

**When to break “hope-forward”**
- Intentional literary bleakness needs clear positioning and usually leaves the commercial MG hope contract — don’t surprise school-market buyers
- Speculative upper MG may hold high external stakes (monsters, quests) while emotional lens stays kid-scaled — external scale ≠ YA stance
