# YA genre hybrids (romance / fantasy / SF / thriller)

Engine: YA age-band contract + an adult genre engine — voice/agency/stakes of YA stay primary; the co-engine still pays its obligatory moments.

## Light

Job: shelve as YA Fantasy / YA Romance / YA SF / YA Thriller without importing adult heat/violence defaults wholesale.

Contract: teen protagonist; problems relatable to teen life; adolescent POV limitations; co-engine payoffs (magic cost, novum, clock, yearning) still fire.

Must-not-break:
- Voice lock — teen psychic distance; adult condescension kills
- Agency — protagonist causes the turn (adults may help, not steal climax)
- Genre obligation kept — fair-play / magic cost / novum / clock still fire
- Identity / belonging crisis braided with genre plot
- Heat/gore ceilings below adult counterparts unless explicitly NA/crossover
- Comps match **both** age band and co-engine

## Normal

**Category facts (YA parent)**
- Written for ~12–18 emotional experience; market buyers often adults — not an excuse to write adult reflection in a locker
- YA definitions cluster: teen protagonist + adolescent POV + problems of interest to that age
- Content: can hold violence, substances, non-explicit sexuality; full explicit heat usually pushes adult
- Dual promise: coming-of-age pressure **and** genre payoffs (HEA optional in some YA romance markets — know the lane)

**Co-engine checklist**
- Romance: yearning + choice; check YA vs NA heat
- Fantasy/SF: teach rules without textbook; keep wonder or estrangement
- Thriller: dread and clock without adult procedural sludge
- Friendship / found-family pressure as common co-stakes
- Ending leaves a future — hope or hard-won clarity typical of YA contract
- Content audit before “edgy” defaults 

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Adult genre novel with a 17-year-old pasted in | Rewrite stance + agency + peer world |
| YA voice with zero genre payoffs | Pay co-engine obligations |
| NA/adult heat marketed as YA | Re-shelf or cut heat |
| Save-the-world with no peer-scale cost | Braid identity/belonging stakes |

## Hard

**Stance diagnostics (hybrid-specific)**
- Malo-Juvera/Hill cluster: teen center of plot + adolescent point of view with limited understanding — if the hybrid’s lore dumps require adult retrospective narration, you left YA stance
- Speculative YA still carries coming-of-age under worldbuilding weight (Earthsea / Potter / Hunger Games lineages show genre can be primary *marketing* while identity stakes remain load-bearing)
- Adult crossover readership is real (~half+ of YA buyers adult) — still write *for* teen emotional experience if you want the YA shelf

**Co-engine collision table**

| Co-engine | YA keep | Adult default to refuse |
|-----------|---------|-------------------------|
| Romance | Yearning, choice, crush→relationship | Explicit erotic spine as category engine |
| Fantasy | Wonder + cost/limits taught in scene | System bible lectures; grimdark hopelessness as default |
| SF | Novum that pressures identity/belonging | Hard-SF problem sets with no peer heartbeat |
| Thriller | Clock + dread at total teen scale | Procedural sludge; torture detail as prestige |

**When to break / re-shelf**
- If autonomy, sexuality, and cynicism match NA/adult → or adult
- If belonging/friendship/family dominate and romance/heat vanish → check upper MG 
- If genre plot ignores coming-of-age entirely → adult genre with teen POV (often mis-shelved)
