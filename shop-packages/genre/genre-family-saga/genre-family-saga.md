# Multi-generational family saga

Engine: dynasty time-scale — inheritance of conflict, property, secrets, and temperament across generations; history enters through the family doorway; no single protagonist owns the whole meaning.

## Light

Job: make inheritance across generations the spine — each era distinct; history pressures the clan, not wallpaper.

Contract: chronicle lives/doings of a family (or interconnected families) over time; in serious intent, a thematic device for historical events, social change, or fortune’s ebb from multiple perspectives.

Must-not-break:
- Inheritance map: money, land, names, trauma, talent
- Each generation needs distinct want — not clone protagonists
- Historical events as pressure, not wallpaper
- Recurring motifs/objects as batons across time
- Cast control: family tree in the drawer; limit active POVs per section
- Timeline consistency (ages/dates)

## Normal

**Genre shape**

- Typical form: generations through a historical period, often a sequence of novels
- Subgenres include AGA saga; popular commercial writers (Howatch, Delderfield, Cookson/Carr…) and literary monuments (*Buddenbrooks*, *Forsyte*, immigrant/estate sagas; *One Hundred Years of Solitude* adjacency)
- Etymology note: English *saga* borrowed via Old Norse prose narratives — expectation of structured long account, not soap-only accumulation
- Can be contemporary-set across decades without “costume history” if social change still enters through the family door

**Working checklist**

- Founding wound that echoes in descendants
- Property / name / secret as throughline object
- Wedding/funeral/war hinge chapters that rebind the clan
- One generation’s victory as another’s prison
- Late revelation that reinterprets early books/acts
- Structure: strict chronology vs braided timelines; who narrates which era

**Obligatory pressures / moments**
- Dynasty time jump
- Founding wound echo
- Throughline object (property/name/secret)
- Hinge ritual/war chapter
- Generational irony (victory → prison)
- Late reinterpretation

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Telephone-book cast | Limit active POVs; spine inheritance |
| One interesting generation | Distinct want per era |
| Soap without argument | Thematic throughline object |
| Timeline soup | Age/date ledger off-page |

## Hard

**Literary monument vs commercial saga**

- Serious intent uses multi-perspective chronicle for historical/social change — commercial AGA/estate sagas may emphasize romance and fortune swings; both still need inheritance spine
- Sequence vs single long novel changes cast control and cliff strategy 

**History-through-family dial**

- History-as-wallpaper fails the serious contract — events must alter inheritance options
- Immigrant / minority sagas (*Pachinko*, *Homegoing* adjacency in lists) make state/racial systems the dynasty antagonist — research ethics apply
