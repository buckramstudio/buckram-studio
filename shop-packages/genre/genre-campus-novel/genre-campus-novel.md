# Campus / academic novel

Engine: university as closed microcosm — hierarchies, pretension vs appetite, students vs faculty, ideas as status weapons; idiosyncratic characters in unambiguous ranks.

## Light

Job: use the university microcosm — ranks, terms, and ideas-as-status — as the social engine.

Contract: closed environment (department, dorm, college town); status economy; comic or serious treatment; ideas dramatized through vanity and desire.

Must-not-break:
- Exploit closed environment: tenure, housing, dining, conferences
- Seasonality clocks: term times, exams, hiring cycles
- Ideas through character want — not lecture alone
- Steel-man intellectual passion — avoid only sneering at scholars
- Students are people, not only sex/plot devices
- Don’t make campus interchangeable with any office (waste the microcosm)

## Normal

**Genre shape**
- Faculty-centered academic novel vs student-centered “varsity” novel (sometimes split)
- Comic/satirical tradition strong (Amis *Lucky Jim*, Lodge) alongside serious treatment (Snow, Coetzee, Roth…)
- Subgenre: campus murder mystery (closed circle like Golden Age house)
- Setting may be real or invented university

**Obligatory pressures / moments**
- Institution microcosm — department, dorm, college town rules
- Status economy — tenure, grades, cliques, patronage
- Scandal or idea that splits the community
- Mentor/rival academic relationship turn
- Public ritual (lecture, ball, hearing, reunion)
- Exile or belonging ending inside/outside the gates

**Working checklist**
- New social attitudes colliding with fixed institutional culture
- Ideas dramatized through vanity and desire
- Closed-circle mystery optional — still needs academic texture if billed campus
- Tone: satire vs sincerity declared early

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Score-settling roman à clef without story | Want + turn |
| Jargon fog | Translate through POV stakes |
| Students as only sex/plot devices | Interior agendas |
| Campus = any office | Use term clocks, ranks, ideas-as-status |

## Hard

**Faculty vs varsity split**
- Faculty satire often targets pretension, tenure, and departmental warfare
- Student-centered novels tilt toward bildungsroman / first independence — may stack or if age-band heat/agency leads
