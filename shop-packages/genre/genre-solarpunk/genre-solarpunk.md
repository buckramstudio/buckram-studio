# Solarpunk

Engine: **solutions taken to radical conclusion** — sustainable, community-scaled futures (renewables, mutual aid, ecological repair) imagined as achievable; optimism without denying crisis.

## Light

Job: dramatize livable redesign — hope as structure, not epilogue wallpaper.

Contract: SF / activist-adjacent futures where appropriate tech serves coexistence; collective agency; problems acknowledged, solutions dramatized; destination is not dystopia (struggle en route OK).

Must-not-break:
- Hope is structural — community, repair, ingenuity load-bearing
- Technology serves coexistence, not alienation chrome
- Collective agency over solitary Chosen One saves Earth
- Show setbacks and labor — don’t greenwash luxury roofs
- Prefigurative texture (gardens, commons, microgrids) feels *practiced*
- Aesthetic greenwash without justice or ecology fails the punk

## Normal

**Genre shape**

- Literary / artistic / activist movement + SF subgenre: what does a sustainable civilization look like, and how do we get there?
- “Solar” = renewables + bright futurity rejecting climate doomerism; “punk” = DIY, countercultural, often post-capitalist / decolonial politics
- Term from ~2008 blog (“From Steampunk to Solarpunk”); visual tumblr wave ~2014; *A Solarpunk Manifesto* (~2019); SFE entry 2020s
- Not required to depict literal solar panels — renewable / appropriate tech / ecological integration broadly
- SFE: “rebellion against a rebellion, born out of dystopia fatigue” — stretch **solutions** instead of “if this goes on” nightmares
- Precursors often claimed: Le Guin, Callenbach *Ecotopia*, Robinson *Pacific Edge*, Spinrad *Songs from the Stars*; Chambers Monk & Robot as explicit contemporary lane

**Core contract**

1. Hope is structural
2. Technology serves coexistence
3. Collective agency
4. Problems acknowledged, solutions dramatized
5. Prefigurative texture practiced on the page

**Obligatory pressures**

- Broken inheritance (climate damage, extractive leftover, failed central authority)
- Practice of alternative at small scale
- Stress test of the commons
- Collective response (negotiation, labor, redesign)
- Tradeoff named — equity is the craft problem
- Forward image: ending enlarges the possible future

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Luxury eco-roofs, no justice | Cost the fix; show who labors |
| Lone carbon-hero | Scale to mutual aid / commons governance |
| Cynicism default | Keep obstacles; change destination affect |
| Fake solarpunk urbanism | Ecology + social justice in the same scene |

## Hard

**Solarpunk vs cli-fi vs cyberpunk**

| | Solarpunk | Cli-fi | Cyberpunk |
|--|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Default affect | Constructive hope | Variable (thriller→lit→grim) | Cynical / neon noir |
| Climate | Often present; focus on livable redesign | Climate as central pressure | Optional backdrop |
| Tech role | Appropriate / renewable / shared | Adaptation or failure modes | Corp control / body chrome |
| Ending grain | Better world imaginable | Contain, adapt, or witness | Survive the rigged city |

**Manifesto / SFE edge cases**

- Manifesto line: never dystopian as the genre’s *destination* — struggle en route OK; works that engage solarpunk ideologies inside dystopian settings (SFE cites Okorafor *Noor*, Zhang *Land of Milk and Honey*) are hybrids — name the promise
- Breadth problem: unlike diesel/steam named after energy signal, solarpunk need not show solar panels — test is radicalized solutions + political grain
- Overlap with hopepunk affect; distinct job is **ecological / infrastructural imagination**

**When to break “never dystopian”**
- Depict dystopia as inheritance being dismantled — still sell redesign as the engine
- Pure collapse witness without redesign → bill as cli-fi / dystopia, not solarpunk
