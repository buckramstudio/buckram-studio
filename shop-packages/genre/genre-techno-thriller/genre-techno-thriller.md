# Techno-thriller

Engine: **plausible near-future (or present) technology as the stakes machine** — thriller pacing + exceptional technical specificity; the plot turns on *how the tech actually works*.

Primary home for **hacker / cyberattack / surveillance** stories on the Clancy / Crichton / crypto-thriller shelf. Neon-noir megacity dystopia is **not** required — when aesthetic + corp dystopia dominate, route to . Parent pace → . Espionage tradecraft → . SF novum depth → .

## Light

Job: make technical particulars the suspense hinge — competence under a clock, not chrome wallpaper.

Contract: thriller narrative structure + exceptional technical detail (often military, networks, labs, aerospace); world stays recognizably ours; victory is usually containment, not remaking civilization.

Must-not-break:
- Tech particulars turn the plot (protocol, exploit, pathogen vector, launch sequence…)
- Plausible near-now or near-future — handwave less than space opera
- Stakes scale outward (city / nation / markets / grid)
- Competence porn with failure modes — not magic “I’m in”
- If future science *transforms* the world as subject → you may be in SF

## Normal

**Genre shape**
- Hybrid of thriller + SF devices + often spy / military / disaster — distinguished by **technical density**
- Type examples: Clancy (*Hunt for Red October*), Crichton (*Andromeda Strain*); Craig Thomas *Firefox* often cited as early innovator
- Usually **undisplaced / mundane world**; Cold War lens historically; now cyber, biotech, AI, infrastructure
- SFE: science is threatening or fetishized; world-transforming implications often **evaded** — novum reduced to McGuffin; End of the World threatened, almost never completed

**Core contract**
1. Competence porn with a clock
2. Tech particulars turn the plot
3. Stakes scale outward
4. Plausibility lane
5. Victory is containment — world remains recognizably ours

**Varieties**

| Variety | Focus |
|---------|--------|
| **Military** | Platforms, doctrine, C2; Clancy-lineage |
| **Spy** | Tradecraft + tech gear; defeat rival program |
| **Crypto / online** | Drama unfolds largely on networks — *primary home for hacker stories* |
| **Disaster** | Tech-mediated catastrophe; survive / contain at scale |
| **SF techno-thriller** | Heavier speculative science still sold as thriller (*Jurassic*-adjacent lab threat) |

**Hacker / cyberattack parking rule**

Park here when setting is near-now, conflict is intrusion/exfil/ransomware/SCADA/deepfake, tone is procedural thriller. Cross to when megacorp cosmology, body/identity chrome, and dystopian tech-noir aesthetic dominate.

**Obligatory pressures**
- Capability demo → breach/discovery (clock starts) → expertise under fire → cascade → countermeasure race → containment climax

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Spec sheets without Want | Attach human cost to every system |
| Magic hacking | Constraints, cost, foreshadowed gotchas |
| Cyberpunk costume on Clancy plot | Match promise to world/aesthetic |
| Permanent world remake, “headlines” blurb | Own the SF shelf |

## Hard

**Technothriller vs SF (SFE test)**
- Techno: undisplaced mundane world; tech as threat/fetish; transforming implications left unexamined; novum often McGuffin
- SF: future developments in science play a central *analytic* role and transform the world as subject
- Same topic (AI, biotech, weapons) can sit either shelf — **promise and world** decide comps

**Information & paranoia overlay**
- Overlaps conspiracy and apocalyptic modes — keep technical hinge load-bearing or retag 
- Bond-adjacent spy-fi gear fetish is common variety; tradecraft depth still belongs with when human network > specs

**When to break “containment only”**
- End-of-world threat can hang over the plot — completing it usually exits the commercial techno contract into apocalypse/SF
- Near-future tech beyond present capacity OK if still plausible extrapolation, not secondary-world physics
