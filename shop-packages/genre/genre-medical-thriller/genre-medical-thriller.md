# Medical thriller

Engine: body, hospital, research lab, or public-health system as threat stage; specialized knowledge creates asymmetric power; conspiracy or malpractice often braids with a ticking clinical clock.

## Light

Job: make clinical/institutional knowledge the asymmetric weapon — survival/exposure is the plot question.

Contract: events center on medical environments, practice, or research; distinct from medical romance/drama (relationship is not the primary engine).

Must-not-break:
- Accuracy floor high enough to avoid false institutional practice — then dramatize
- Clock: lab results, incubation, OR window, hearing deadlines
- Expertise as both weapon and vulnerability
- Ethical dilemmas on the page (consent, triage, experimentation)
- Patients humanized — bodies not only gore props

## Normal

**Genre shape**

- Lineage touchstone: Crichton *Coma* as early medical-thriller landmark; doctor novels + research fiction adjacent
- Modes: hospital chase, bioweapon / epidemic, organ-harvest conspiracy, corrupt pharma/research, psychiatric-institution dread
- Institutional antagonist: hospital admin, regulator, cartel, research funder — name the system

**Working checklist**

- Teach jargon fairly; don’t fog the dramatic question
- Whistleblower / cover-up pressure
- Procedure set-piece with human stakes
- Containment or exposure ending with ethical residue
- Don’t confuse with techno-thriller weapons systems (different engine)

**Obligatory pressures**
- Body/institution threat (outbreak, malpractice, bioweapon, hospital conspiracy)
- Expertise barrier
- Clinical or regulatory clock
- Whistleblower / cover-up
- Procedure set-piece
- Containment or exposure ending

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Technobabble dump | One dramatic question per procedure |
| Miracle science midbook | Lock rules early; cost breaks |
| Malpractice porn | Thematic/ethical cost |
| Wrong engine | Near-future systems weapons → techno-thriller |
