# Horror fiction

Engine: dread, not gore. Reader should feel unsafe.

## Light

Job: make anticipation and aftermath cost scarier than the creature reveal.

Contract: isolation + violated safe space + threat rules; terror (anticipation) outranks jump-scare and gore-as-fake-escalation.

Must-not-break:
- Dread > jump scares — anticipation of the threat scarier than the threat
- Establish threat “rules” early; violate once at climax
- Isolation: physical, social, or psychological
- Violate the safe space (home, body, mind)
- What you don’t describe > what you do
- Aftermath cost — what cannot be recovered

## Normal

**Terror / horror / revulsion (gothic lineage)**
- **Terror** — anticipation, sublime dread, imagination stretched
- **Horror** — confrontation with the awful thing
- **Revulsion** — disgust / bodily reaction
- Gothic toolkit still feeds modern horror: haunted space, ancestral guilt, doubles, liminal night, sublime landscape → 

**Core model — three pillars before the monster**
1. **Investment** — care about someone before fearing for them
2. **Uncertainty** — unknown *or* known-and-helpless
3. **Stakes** — identity, belonging, family, safety-in-the-world — not only pulse

**Working checklist / dread techniques**
- Off-page threat; long fuse; denial delay; sensory wrongness; liminal spaces
- Pacing: long slow tension → short sharp bursts → longer tension again
- Shorten sentences as tension rises; ban vague “horrible/indescribable” — force specific sensory demand
- Arc: normalcy + intrusion → escalation + denial → confrontation + **aftermath cost**
- Subplot as thematic mirror (haunting = grief; possession = lost self)

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Over-explain monster | Implication; cut last two descriptive sentences of reveal |
| Gore as fake escalation | Raise uncertainty / stakes instead |
| Kill unknowns early | Preserve mystery until earned |
| Villain taxonomy monologue | Ordeal + cost, not lecture |
| Neglect warm ordinary world | Horror power ∝ value of what’s destroyed |

## Hard

**Uncanny & psychological**
- Freud’s *unheimlich*: familiar made strange (dolls, doubles, automata, homes that aren’t)
- Psychological horror: mind/perception/identity collapse — unreliable perception; mundane → sinister; core emotion first (guilt, shame, grief), then uncanny beats that press that nerve
- Payoff reshapes reality; tidy taxonomic explanations kill residual dread

**Body horror toolkit (sibling)**
- Violation of bodily integrity/autonomy; start small → escalate; empathy > pure revulsion; medical plausibility anchors; themes of shame/recognition

**When to break “rules early”**
- Cosmic mode: rules exist to show how little they protect — still teach the *human* procedure before it fails
- Ambiguous gothic doubt vs confirmed entity: dual-readable signs only if that is the promise
- Complicity: protagonist’s coherent wrong choice pulls reader into participation — stronger than random victimization
