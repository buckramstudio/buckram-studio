# Police procedural

Engine: investigation as **system work** — team, protocol, error, paperwork, and the gap between what TV promises and what jurisdictions allow.

## Light

Job: show how the justice machine works — gather → evaluate → hypothesize → build a case that can survive court.

Contract: crime solved by members of the criminal justice system; often ensemble/series; differs from cozy amateur intuition and lone hardboiled PI.

Must-not-break:
- Unit roles clear (scene / interview / press ownership)
- Evidence trail could hypothetically reach court
- Personal stake for lead beyond paycheck
- Procedure varies by jurisdiction — generic “cop show” lore fails
- Recaps dramatized, not dumped

## Normal

**Genre shape**

- Emphasis on **how** the machine works; multiple cases or dead ends common
- Readers often return for cast as much as case

**Craft tips readers reward**

1. Cast first — protagonist + supporting unit
2. Maintain the mystery — hook early; every scene answers or asks
3. Plot visibly — timelines, maps, scene cards
4. Research late enough — draft with flags; license OK if rule-breaking has consequences
5. Clean prose — crisp, followable
6. Pace with micro-conflict — small obstacles every scene
7. Recap dilemma — briefing dumps kill; short, interesting location, exit on a bang

**Authenticity without a training manual**

- Know **why** procedures exist (rights, evidence integrity, public trust) — then bend intelligently
- Chain of custody, documentation, lab backlog — use when they create pressure
- Contemporary forensics must match era (DNA queues ≠ instant magic)
- Rogue detective still needs a path that could produce a conviction — or show shortcut cost
- Prefer primary/local sources over crime-show osmosis

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| TV osmosis procedure | Jurisdiction-specific constraints |
| Instant forensics magic | Backlog / prioritization pressure |
| “Because it’s my job” only | Personal stake + mystery hook |
| Recap dump | Dramatize; exit on new pressure |

## Hard

**Fair-play vs machine realism**

- Puzzle-first closed circle → lean / cozy
- System error, workload, false trails as pleasure → stay procedural
- When the file moves to court strategy → 

**Noir tension inside the badge**

- Law vs justice gap can live inside a unit without abandoning procedure
- Shortcuts that smash chain-of-custody must cost convictions or careers

**When to break ensemble focus**
- Lone-wolf episodes OK if the machine still constrains outcomes
- Compress dull bureaucracy for story — stay believable, not documentary-complete
