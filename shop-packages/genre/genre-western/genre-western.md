# Western

Engine: landscape shapes character; law vs freedom at the edge of civilization. Cost of order measured in what gets destroyed along the way.

## Light

Job: make frontier psychology load-bearing — isolation, distance from legal authority, landscape that dwarfs human presence.

Contract: if characters think like modern suburbanites in period hats, no amount of period detail saves it. Neo-Western updates infrastructure, not the psychology.

Must-not-break:
- Landscape pressure on every major decision
- Protagonist’s code stated and tested
- External conflict + internal (code, demons, belonging)
- Period tech/economy constraints (travel time, communication lag)
- Antagonist with understandable motives — not only black-hat silhouette
- Avoid one-note tropes without psychological specificity

## Normal

**Genre shape**

- Classic: American Old West frontier (roughly late 18th–19th c.)
- Lineage: Cooper → dime novels → pulps → Grey/L’Amour → revisionist/literary (McMurtry, McCarthy, Portis…)
- Modes: traditional, adult Western, neo-Western, weird/SF/horror Western hybrids

**Frontier psychology**

- Isolation and scarce help shape decisions
- Neo-Western (*No Country for Old Men*, *Hell or High Water*): same psychology, updated infrastructure
- Weird West: supernatural works because the frontier is already frightening — fuse without one genre swallowing the other

**Plot & conflict**

- Setting as character: harsh beauty creates atmosphere and tension
- Themes: justice vs law, civilization vs wilderness, community vs individualism, cost of violence
- Build toward showdown without rushing; moral situations where right/wrong aren’t clean
- Balance action with quiet character beats — action reveals character under pressure
- Ending can resolve main conflict while leaving frontier ambiguity

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Period hats, suburban mind | Rebuild decision logic from isolation |
| Black-hat only | Motives the frontier explains |
| Rushed showdown | Escalate obstacles; quiet beats |
| Costume detail, no landscape pressure | Distance/weather/scarce resources on choices |

## Hard

**Traditional vs neo vs weird**

- Neo-Western: keep frontier psychology; change infrastructure
- Weird West: fuse without swallowing — dread must use frontier fear
- Contemporary western sibling → 

**Adventure overlap**

- Journey/quest pleasure may share set pieces — law-vs-freedom / landscape-as-character still bill first for Western

**When to break classic geography**
- Relocate the psychology (borderlands, modern rural) if isolation + weak authority remain
- Revisionist endings may refuse clean justice — seed the moral fog
