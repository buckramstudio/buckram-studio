# Middle grade craft

Fiction for ~8–12 (core often 9–12): wonder, friendship/family engines, kid agency, hope — not teen YA shelved young.

## Light

Job: deliver kid-scaled stakes with authentic voice, humor+heart, and protagonist agency — adults may help but do not solve the core problem.

Contract: protagonists usually ~10–13; belonging/friendship/family/competence central; romance at most crush/blush/maybe first kiss.

Must-not-break:
- Voice: direct, present-focused, concrete — no talking down, baby talk, or adult philosopher in a backpack
- Friendship often more central than romance
- Treat kid-scale stakes (friendship fracture, embarrassment, unfair rules) with full seriousness
- Hope / agency thread even in darker MG
- Adults present and real; protagonist drives resolution
- Gatekeepers (parents, teachers, librarians) affect shelf access — craft constraint

## Normal

**Category boundaries (MG ≠ YA-lite)**

- Readers ~8–12; often **read up** (protagonists slightly older)
- YA pivot: identity/autonomy/romance heat, adult-scale moral labyrinth 
- Speculative MG can hold high external stakes while emotional lens stays kid-scaled

**Voice without talking down**

- Older-friend narrator energy, not teacher/parent sermon
- Prefer first person or close third; multi-POV rare / limited in upper MG
- Read aloud: condescending or costume-kid → revise

**Humor + stakes**

- Humor from character’s angle + heart; bathroom-only or mean humiliation as humor fails
- External danger OK; avoid graphic injury, sadistic violence, hopeless endings as default
- Hard topics: problem→path craft, not trauma tourism 
- Avoid adult glib cynicism as worldview default

**Friendship, family, length, agency**

- Word-count bands (guides): younger ~20–40k; mid ~30–50k; upper ~40–55k; fantasy/SF often longer
- Hook fast; shorter chapters than adult norms; manageable cast (~4–6 core)
- Protagonist makes key decisions; passive observer or adult-rescued Act 3 = category friction

| Symptom | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Sounds YA | Soften heat/cynicism; recenter friendship/family |
| Sounds adult-in-kid-suit | Present focus; cut reflective distance |
| Talks down | Trust intelligence; cut over-explain |
| Soft/preachy | Story first; theme via plot |
| Adults solve climax | Return agency to kid + peers |
| Graphic / hopeless | Recast for MG contract |
| Wrong length | Re-home chapter book vs upper MG vs YA |

## Hard

**Gatekeeper vs kid reader**

- Discovery often runs through adults — content choices affect access without becoming sermon
- Upper MG edge : more complexity, still not YA heat/autonomy pivot

**When to break “hope default”**
- Darker MG can end bittersweet if agency and meaning remain — hopeless void usually breaks the shelf
- Speculative high body-count: keep emotional lens and aftermath kid-scaled, not adult cynicism
