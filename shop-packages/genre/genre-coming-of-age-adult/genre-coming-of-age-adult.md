# Coming-of-age (adult)

Engine: identity formation after (or beside) the YA shelf — first career, migration, divorce, sobriety, art vocation, late diagnosis; the self re-authored under adult consequences.

## Light

Job: re-author the self under adult institutions — not teen CoA with aged-up slang.

Contract: identity question (who am I if not X?); threshold events with irreversible adult cost; ending = provisional new self, not fake total completion.

Must-not-break:
- Name the identity question early
- Adult institutions bind (job, law, family trade, art world) — consequences stick
- Avoid resetting to teen psychology without cause
- Distinct from YA shelf voice/agency 
- Distinct from NA first-independence marketing when midlife/role-shift leads 
- Ending in practice, not prom

## Normal

**Genre shape**
- Bildungsroman / Künstlerroman lineages often adult-marketed even when youth-centered
- Adult CoA: protagonist may be 20s–40s+; “coming of age” = role shift, not only puberty
- Coming-of-age novel sometimes used more loosely than technical *bildungsroman*
- Classic plot outline (criticism): emotional loss → journey into the world → conflict with society → gradual, difficult maturity (acceptance or hard-won renegotiation)

**Obligatory pressures / moments**
- Old role dies (child, spouse, job, hometown self)
- Mentor or foil who embodies a possible future
- Humiliation or failure that teaches the Need
- Threshold act claiming the new self in public
- Aftermath — adult CoA ends in practice

**Working checklist**
- Society’s apprenticeship system visible (job, art world, family trade)
- Sex/work/parenting stakes allowed without YA guardrails
- Avoid passive montage without cost
- Mentor is not a lecture machine

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| YA voice in adult billing | Adult consequence + stance |
| Passive montage | Costed threshold scenes |
| Mentor as lecture | Foil with conflicting Want |
| Midlife crisis novelty only | Identity question + practice ending |

## Hard

**Named variants (retag / stack)**
- **Entwicklungsroman** — general growth rather than self-cultivation
- **Erziehungsroman** — training and formal schooling focus
- **Künstlerroman** — artist’s development (*Portrait of the Artist…* lineage)
- Moretti-linked tension: modernity’s overvaluation of youth/progress vs teleological “happiness/reconciliation” endings — useful when endings feel false or forced

**Shelf edges**
- First independence ~18–29 with romance/heat market → often 
- Teen institutional frame → 
- Campus as apprenticeship machine → may stack 

**When to break “society accepts the protagonist” classic ending**
- Refusal / exile / chosen outsiderhood can still complete adult CoA if the new self is practiced on-page
- Fake total completion is the failure mode — provisional self is the honest landing
