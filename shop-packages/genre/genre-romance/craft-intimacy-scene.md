# Intimacy / sex scene craft

Story function of sex and intimacy — beyond word bans. Heat level is a craft choice; emotional change is mandatory. Adult consensual intimacy craft.

Language bans / anatomy realism / erotica traps → . Engines → , . Couple / yearning → .

## Light

Job: make intimacy a scene with purpose — revelation, relationship shift, plot consequence, or arc beat — not a quota.

Contract: heat level matches shelf/cover promise; after the scene, something between them (or in the plot) has changed.

Must-not-break:
- At least one strong story reason (prefer two)
- Clear want / consent on the page (unless the story is *about* coercion — then genre honesty)
- Character voice does not vanish into purple thesaurus
- Aftermath carries change — don’t skip payoff
- Heat contract honored (sweet ≠ sudden explicit; “spicy” ≠ perpetual fade)

## Normal

**Purpose first (sex is a scene)**
Good reasons (need at least one strong; prefer two):
- **Character revelation** — who they are when vulnerable / messy / controlling / playful
- **Relationship shift** — trust, power, belonging, “something to lose”
- **Plot consequence** — confession, witnessed act, pregnancy, betrayal, alliance
- **Arc beat** — risk taken, Lie challenged, Need approached
- **Conflict** — wrong person/time/reason; complicates next chapters
- **Theme** — power, healing, corruption, connection embodied

Weak reasons: quota (“been X chapters”); genre checkbox with no local purpose; titillation alone; proving attraction already established.

Cut test: “If I remove this scene, what specific story loss?” Vague answer → cut, fade, or reframe.

**Heat levels (contract with reader)**

| Level | What reader gets | Typical homes |
|-------|------------------|---------------|
| **Fade / closed door** | Build-up + cut + aftermath | Thriller, cozy, some fantasy, YA, author comfort |
| **Sensual / warm** | Emotion + sensation; minimal mechanics | Mainstream / sweet romance, romantic subplot |
| **Explicit / hot** | Clear acts; body + feeling balanced | Most contemporary romance, romantasy heat |
| **Erotic / scorching** | Graphic centrality; sex *is* plot engine | Erotica / erotic romance |

- Violating contract breaks trust
- Explicitness ≠ quality; emotion + consequence can outrank mechanics
- Write the heat you can inhabit — discomfort shows as purple, clinical, or flinching prose
- Genre survey: read comps for language density and page count of heat

**Fade-to-black as craft (not cowardice)**
- Still a scene: charged before + meaningful after
- Before: looks, decision, consent signal, what they risk
- Cut at escalation peak or door-close; resume on emotional/plot aftermath
- Aftermath carries the change (uncertainty; new alliance; regret; joy)
- Use when intimacy *matters* more than choreography — or heat contract forbids on-page sex
- Don’t use fade to dodge consequence; the cut still costs something

**Emotional function & wound**
Ask per scene:
1. Because of this moment, what changes between them?
2. What emotional risk is present *right now*?
3. What does the reader learn that couldn’t show another way?
4. Would this still matter if the physical acts were off-page?

- Intimacy isn’t created by anatomy lists — by **perception** (POV lens, sensory filter, specificity)
- Quiet emotional states often land harder than gymnastic choreography: fear, trust, grief, relief, shame, wonder
- Tie to wound/shielding: does this threaten or soothe the unmet need? 
- Internal thoughts during = relationship/plot engine, not therapy lecture mid-act

**Consent and power on the page**
- Ambiguity of *want* is usually a craft failure (unless story is *about* coercion — then label/genre honesty)
- Clear want: verbal check-in **or** unmistakable enthusiastic participation (pull closer, initiate, guide)
- Power: who initiates, who leads, who yields, who reverses — reveal character; shifts mid-scene = drama
- Consent theater (legal-contract speeches, rote formula) ≠ embodied consent 
- YA / teen-adjacent: readiness, respect, consequence matter more than spice count
- Dark / dubious dynamics: intentional genre contract; never accidental

**Character-specific desire**
- Orderly character → same in bed, or rare chaos? That’s character data
- Voice doesn’t vanish: analytical notices; guarded resists while participating; bold directs
- Desire is particular: what *this* person wants from *this* partner tonight (comfort, conquest, oblivion, proof, play)
- Same acts, different meaning by relationship stage (first trust ≠ established ritual ≠ breakup sex)
- Imperfection humanizes: fumble, laugh, adjust, check-in — perfection reads fake
- Not every scene must be “sexy”; awkward, funny, tender, tense, or sad can be correct

**Pacing & architecture**
**Before:** emotional entry state; anticipation earned; consent signal; stakes of risking this
**During:** physical + emotional braid; sensory grounding; power moves; rhythm via sentence length
**After:** processing; relationship/plot consequence; bridge to next pressure — don’t skip payoff

- Longing / hesitation / small charged gestures do heavy lifting before the scene
- Rush = hollow; slog of redundant motion = skim — usually 1–2 pages sensual/explicit enough unless erotica centerpiece
- Short sentences: urgency; longer: tenderness / exploration; mix for rise–peak–fall
- Scene has rise and fall like any other; something must *happen* beyond bodies colliding

**Language & tone (craft, not ban list)**
- Match vocabulary to the rest of the book — sex-scene thesaurus eruptions = Bad Sex Award territory
- Fruit metaphors, “throbbing manhood,” sudden purple = embarrassment hiding
- Experience from inside POV (breath catch, nerve demand) > external anatomical report
- Dialogue/thought can be surprising, filthy, practical, or silent — character-true
- For ban lists / motion clichés / fluid exaggeration → 

| Symptom | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Hot but forgettable | Name the change; strengthen after |
| Clinical | More POV sensation + emotion; less inventory |
| Purple / cringe | Same lexicon as rest of book; cut metaphors |
| Consent muddy | Enthusiastic participation or clear words |
| Wrong heat for shelf | Fade or escalate to match contract |
| No aftermath | Add processing + consequence beat |
| Identical every pairing | Character-specific desire + power pattern |

## Hard

**Heat × shelf cross-lane**
- Romance category: intimacy usually serves relationship arc + HEA/HFN honesty 
- Erotica: sex may *be* the plot engine — still needs local turn; serial identical acts = skim
- Thriller / mystery: closed door often correct; if on-page, expect plot consequence (witness, blackmail, alliance)
- YA / age-band: prioritize readiness, respect, consequence over spice density
- Dark romance / dubious consent: market labeling first — accidental ambiguity is a craft fail

**Power-shift diagnostics**
| Mid-scene shift | What it can mean |
|-----------------|------------------|
| Follower → director | Trust arrival; Essence peek |
| Play → withdrawal | Wound trigger; black-moment seed |
| Matched → imbalance | Conflict for next chapter; not always abuse — name the after-feeling |
| Performance → honesty | Mask drops ( Identity→Essence) |

**When fade beats explicit (decision fork)**
- Story loss is emotional/plot, not choreography knowledge
- Author or audience contract forbids graphic detail
- POV character dissociates / cannot process — aftermath may be more honest than mechanics
- Still write the before/after with full scene pressure

**When to break “always aftermath processing”**
- Immediate external interrupt (raid, discovery) can *be* the consequence — process later, but don’t erase cost
- Series of encounters in erotica may batch reflection — each scene still needs a local micro-turn
- Comedy beat may land on laugh not pillow talk — tonal match required
