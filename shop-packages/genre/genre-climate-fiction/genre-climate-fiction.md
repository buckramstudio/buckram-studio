# Climate fiction (cli-fi)

Engine: **anthropogenic climate pressure as plot weather** — changed or changing climate reshapes daily life, choice, and power; science-grounded speculation, not generic disaster chrome.

## Light

Job: make climate a decision-forcer — body-scale weather plus human systems (policy, markets, migration, insurance).

Contract: climate system + human systems are load-bearing; span thriller/lit/YA/utopia/dystopia — climate is pressure, not a single shelf tone; trade-offs on every adaptation.

Must-not-break:
- Climate as decision-forcer, not wallpaper
- Mundane signals (smoke days, rationing, insurance refusal) earn the world
- Emotional journey central — not a white paper
- Solutions/adaptations cost someone
- Avoid lone genius saves the climate (scale mismatch)
- Lecture / chart dump that pauses story fails

## Normal

**Genre shape**

- Fiction dealing with **climate change** (often human-caused), near future / present / secondary world under climate stress
- Term **cli-fi** popularized by Dan Bloom (~2007–08); literary conversation accelerated 2010s; Robinson *Ministry for the Future* as high-visibility landmark
- Precursors/retroactive: Ballard climate disasters, Butler *Parable* books, Atwood MaddAddam, Herbert *Dune* (ecology), etc.
- Distinct from weather-of-convenience or pure natural-disaster thrillers when **climate + human systems** are load-bearing
- Ghosh’s “great derangement” critique: realist forms that can’t include climate fail imaginatively — craft implication: make nature an **agent**, not backdrop

**Obligatory pressures**

- Baseline → breach (lived climate normal, then irreversible/escalating moment)
- Body-scale scene (heat, smoke, flood, thirst — sensory, not statistic)
- System collision (housing / food / health / border / insurance)
- Trade-off choice (adaptation helps some, harms others)
- Community fracture or coalition
- Knowledge scene woven into struggle, not dumped
- Optional hopeful toolkit: cartographic specificity ecological thinking **communitarian** action

**Worldbuilding notes**

- Pick **one primary climate pressure** + **one human system disrupted** for the spine; layer others as texture
- Consistency: if the world has two moons, it always has two
- Weather as continuous character pressure even when plot is interpersonal
- Avoid jargon walls: cause-and-effect in sensory and social terms
- Data feeds: 

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Lecture / chart dump | Sensory + social cause-and-effect |
| Buzz chasing the issue | Personal urgency + trade-off choice |
| Lone carbon-hero | Collective scale or honest mismatch |
| Vague bad-weather future | Specific pressure + disrupted system |
| Despair porn / empty hope | Choice architecture with cost |

## Hard

**Cli-fi vs solarpunk vs dystopia**

| | Cli-fi | Solarpunk | Dystopian climate |
|--|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| Engine | Climate pressure on life/choice/power | Radicalized sustainable redesign | Social order weaponizes scarcity/control |
| Affect | Variable | Constructive hope default | Oppressive system grain |
| Ending | Contain, adapt, witness, or redesign | Better world imaginable | Survival under regime |

**Craft edge cases**

- Research-grounded ranges as settings bible — then dramatize; don’t pause for white paper
- Hopeful toolkit is optional mode inside cli-fi, not a requirement — solarpunk if redesign is the *primary* promise
- Nature-as-agent: weather continuous pressure even in interpersonal plots (Ghosh implication)

**When to break “science-grounded”**
- Secondary-world climate OK if internal consistency holds
- Pure disaster chrome without human systems → thriller/disaster, not cli-fi
